# Title decision for the feasibility-first manuscript

**Current title**

> How Many Categories Are Enough? Distribution-Free Certification Limits for Few-Shot Anomaly Thresholds

## Semantic audit

The current title is engaging and contains the central search terms, but the word
“enough” can be read as promising a sufficient category count. The broad result in
Proposition 3 supplies necessary all-zero lower limits for a stated deterministic,
uniformly valid, distribution-free procedure class. It does not show that 14, 29,
or 59 categories suffice for CRESS. Proposition 2 gives an exact zero-loss
feasibility calculation for the declared Hoeffding radius, but positive selection
still depends on observed loss, candidate multiplicity, and the transfer assumptions.
The manuscript repeatedly makes this distinction, so the current title is defensible,
but it creates an avoidable first-impression risk.

## Candidate titles

| Candidate | Strength | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How Many Categories Are Enough? Distribution-Free Certification Limits for Few-Shot Anomaly Thresholds** | Engaging question; preserves current metadata and cover letter. | “Enough” may imply a sufficient count that the general lower bound does not provide. |
| **Counting Categories, Not Images: Feasibility Limits for Certified Few-Shot Anomaly Thresholds** | Quotable and foregrounds the central estimand distinction. | “Certified thresholds” may sound as though the strict category route succeeds. |
| **Counting Categories, Not Images: Distribution-Free Feasibility Limits for Few-Shot Anomaly Threshold Certification** | States the unit contrast, procedure class, and feasibility focus without implying successful certification or a sufficient count. | Longer and less conversational. |

## Recommendation

Use the third title for submission:

> **Counting Categories, Not Images: Distribution-Free Feasibility Limits for Few-Shot Anomaly Threshold Certification**

This title best matches the actual evidentiary contribution: the paper studies when
certification can be nonvacuous, not a universally successful certified detector. It
also protects the manuscript against a reviewer interpreting 14/29/59 as sufficient
budgets. The change is editorial rather than scientific and should be applied only
after author approval because it affects `main.tex`, PDF metadata, cover letter,
README, highlights/search positioning, and the immutable release record.
