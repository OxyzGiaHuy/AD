\subsection{Clean Accuracy and Storage}
\input{tables/tab_clean_efficiency}
Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} summarizes clean image-level performance and storage. The official AnomalyDINO reproduction (released code, 3 seeds) leads MVTec image AUROC at every $k$ (0.965/0.976/0.980), which we report as an explicit accuracy reference: neither the controlled nearest-neighbor baseline nor CRR claims ranking superiority on MVTec. CalibSubspaceHead is competitive with the controlled DINOv2 nearest-neighbor memory-bank baseline while being far more compact: PCA64 stores about 0.472 MB, compared with roughly 2--6 MB for the controlled memory bank. On VisA, the detector is stronger relative to this controlled baseline, and the PCA128 ablation improves VisA AUROC by 0.011--0.017 over PCA64 while keeping storage below 0.6 MB. The controlled rows must not be read as official PatchCore or AnomalyDINO reproductions; only the official AnomalyDINO rows are.

\emph{Storage accounting and compute cost.} The per-class figures above cover only the class-specific reference state and deliberately exclude the shared frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone ($\approx$22M parameters, $\approx$84\,MB in fp32), which every compared method requires once per deployment. The 0.472\,MB CRR state decomposes into the PCA subspace (64 components $\times$ 384 dims in fp32 $\approx$ 0.098\,MB, matching the observed 0.094\,MB PCA128--PCA64 increment) and the synthetic-anomaly head and calibrator ($\approx$0.37\,MB); the controlled memory bank stores up to 4096 patch vectors ($\le$6.0\,MB). At inference, cached-feature scoring costs about 1.3\,ms per image for the subspace detector versus 5--13\,ms for the nearest-neighbor bank, while the shared backbone forward dominates end-to-end latency (order of seconds per batch on first pass, identical across methods). Fitting the $k$ leave-one-out subspaces for LOIO adds a one-time per-class setup cost of $k$ PCA fits on at most $k{\times}1369$ patch vectors, i.e., well under a second at $k\le8$.

\subsection{Full VisA Conformal Reliability}
\input{tables/tab_visa_full_conformal}
Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal} gives the main calibration-error result. Throughout the paper, ECE uses 15 equal-width bins; because few-shot conformal confidences occupy at most $k{+}1$ distinct values, we also report an equal-mass adaptive ECE below, and Section~\ref{sec:uniformity} replaces ECE with conformal-native diagnostics for the central claim. Across 56,000 full VisA corruption rows, LOIO conformal obtains pooled ECE 0.0766 while preserving the same PCA/subspace ranking. The strongest regime is $k=4$, where pooled ECE drops to 0.0391. At $k=8$, ECE increases to 0.1140 but remains better than Platt-style baselines in every corruption cell. The uniformity analysis in Section~\ref{sec:uniformity} explains this $k$ trend mechanistically: with a clean support and corrupted test images, normal test residuals are inflated relative to the clean LOIO calibration set; the tighter $k{=}8$ subspace amplifies this mismatch, pushing normal p-values anti-conservative.

\input{tables/tab_visa_ece_by_corruption}
Table~\ref{tab:visa-ece-corruption} isolates corruption effects. LOIO conformal improves over Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt for blur, brightness/contrast shift, Gaussian noise, and JPEG at both $k=4$ and $k=8$. Weighted conformal is a useful ablation but not a safe default: it is competitive for selected $k=8$ structured corruptions, yet it is much worse at $k=4$ and under Gaussian noise.

\subsection{Full MVTec Conformal Reliability}
The same conformal protocol was run on all 15 MVTec classes ($k\in\{4,8\}$, seeds 0--2, four corruptions, 37{,}464 image rows). The VisA conclusion replicates: LOIO conformal reliability attains an overall ECE of 0.0684 (0.0600 at $k{=}4$, 0.0769 at $k{=}8$) while preserving the raw ranking (image AUROC 0.912), and it improves ECE over both Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt in every one of the eight $k$--corruption cells, often by a factor of two to four (e.g., $k{=}4$ Gaussian noise: 0.033 versus 0.233 and 0.270). Weighted conformal is again markedly worse (ECE 0.31--0.45). Conformal reliability is therefore not a VisA-specific effect; it holds across both benchmarks under identical protocols.

\subsection{Statistical Validity: Uniformity Tests, Paired Significance, and Adaptive ECE}
\label{sec:uniformity}
Conformal p-values promise $P(p\le\alpha)\le\alpha$ on normal data under exchangeability---a false-alarm guarantee, not a posterior probability. We therefore test this property directly instead of relying on ECE. Because a $k$-shot LOIO p-value is discrete on the grid $\{j/(k{+}1)\}$, a continuous Kolmogorov--Smirnov test against $U(0,1)$ is invalid by construction; we instead compare the empirical CDF of normal p-values to its exact expectation at every grid point and calibrate the resulting discrete KS statistic against the exact null by Monte Carlo. Uniformity is rejected in all 16 dataset--$k$--corruption cells (Monte Carlo $p\le0.005$), but the \emph{direction} of the violation is the informative result. On VisA at $k{=}4$ the deviation is uniformly conservative (empirical CDF below nominal; worst gap $-0.10$ at the 0.4 grid point), consistent with the below-nominal false-alarm rates above. On VisA at $k{=}8$ and on MVTec at both $k$, the deviation flips anti-conservative under corruption---most severely for Gaussian noise on MVTec $k{=}4$, where $\hat{F}(0.2)=0.46$ against a nominal $0.20$. This is exactly the clean-support/corrupted-test mechanism anticipated by the exchangeability caveat in the method section: corruption inflates normal test residuals relative to the clean LOIO calibration set, and a tighter $k{=}8$ subspace amplifies the mismatch. A matched-LOIO audit that scores support and test under the same fold-specific subspaces (the jackknife+ pairing~\cite{barber2021jackknife}) does not repair this trade-off: on representative MVTec $k{=}4$ it raises clean false alarms from 0.17 to 0.25 with essentially unchanged power. The violation is driven by the support--test condition mismatch, not by the leave-one-out asymmetry alone---which is precisely the failure mode SC3R's matched-condition source validation addresses.

Paired significance replaces point estimates for the headline calibration comparison. Over class--seed--corruption cells, LOIO conformal beats Vector Platt with Wilcoxon signed-rank $p\le5\times10^{-9}$ on both benchmarks and both $k$ (MVTec $k{=}4$: $0.120\pm0.054$ versus $0.223\pm0.095$, LOIO better in 84\% of 360 cells; VisA $k{=}4$: $0.131\pm0.064$ versus $0.286\pm0.060$, better in 97\%). Against Shift-Aware Platt the result holds everywhere except VisA $k{=}8$, where the difference is not significant ($p{=}0.20$, LOIO better in 51\% of cells)---an honest boundary we state rather than average away. Replacing equal-width with equal-mass (adaptive) ECE, which cannot be flattered by at most $k{+}1$ occupied confidence levels, increases LOIO's per-cell ECE (MVTec $k{=}4$: $0.120\to0.160$; VisA $k{=}4$: $0.132\to0.227$) while leaving the ordering against Platt baselines intact. Binning does inflate the apparent margin, which is one more reason the operational analyses below, not ECE, carry the central claim.

\subsection{Attainable Alpha and Operational False-Alarm Control}
\input{tables/tab_attainable_alpha}
Calibration error alone does not answer the operator's question: ``if I alarm at $p\le\alpha$, how often is a good part flagged?'' Table~\ref{tab:attainable-alpha} reports the attainable-alpha analysis. The pattern is structural, not empirical: at $k{=}4$ every nominal level below $0.2$ raises zero alarms on both datasets because no attainable p-value exists below the floor $1/(k{+}1)$, and at $k{=}8$ the same holds below $1/9$. Reporting these zeros as ``conservative coverage'' would be misleading; they are resolution artifacts of the few-shot calibration set.

\input{tables/tab_false_alarm_control}
Table~\ref{tab:false-alarm-control} evaluates the first attainable operating point, $\alpha{=}0.20$, on both full benchmarks. On VisA the LOIO alarm rule is conservative but functional (VisA $k{=}4$: false-alarm 0.14--0.16 with detection 0.58--0.61 and precision around 0.81); on full MVTec the same rule is anti-conservative under corruption at $k{=}4$, which the SC3R analysis below addresses directly. We note a practical pitfall uncovered during this analysis: p-values stored in single precision can exceed their exact rational value (e.g., $1/5\rightarrow0.20000000298$), so an exact comparison $p\le\alpha$ silently discards every alarm at the floor. All thresholding in this paper uses a small tolerance, and we recommend the same in deployments.

\subsection{SC3R: Operating Below the Target-Only Alpha Floor}
\input{tables/tab_sc3r_source_validated}
Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-source-validated} evaluates SC3R against the target-only LOIO anchor on all 15 label-stratified MVTec classes ($k{=}4$, seeds 0--2, five conditions, 675 evaluation cells), under a pre-registered decision gate fixed before the run: nonzero power at $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10\}$, mean false-alarm rate at most $\alpha{+}0.02$, no-harm versus the anchor in at least 80\% of class--seed--corruption cells, no target anomaly labels, and hierarchical class--seed bootstrap support. Matched-condition SC3R passes every criterion. It achieves mean power 0.216 at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and 0.416 at $\alpha{=}0.10$ in a regime where the target-only anchor is structurally silent, while mean false-alarm rates track nominal almost exactly: 0.050, 0.105, and 0.216 at $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, with per-condition false-alarm rates between 0.048 and 0.051 at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and alarm precision above 0.90 throughout. No-harm rates are 89\%, 82\%, and 89\%. The hierarchical class--seed bootstrap is decisive at this scale: the 95\% confidence interval for the power gain over the anchor excludes zero for \emph{every} condition at both sub-floor levels (e.g., clean $\alpha{=}0.10$: $[0.30,0.64]$; JPEG $\alpha{=}0.05$: $[0.02,0.28]$). At $\alpha{=}0.20$, at the anchor's floor, the roles reverse in an instructive way: the anchor attains higher power but its false-alarm rate inflates to 0.27--0.46 under corruption, well above nominal, whereas SC3R remains within 0.005--0.035 of nominal. Source validation thus buys consistent false-alarm tracking at every level, at the cost of some power at levels the anchor can reach. Residual weaknesses are small and localized: JPEG at $\alpha{=}0.10$ (0.121) and Gaussian noise/JPEG at $\alpha{=}0.20$ (0.226/0.235) exceed the $\alpha{+}0.02$ budget per-condition while the pooled means stay within it.

\subsection{ECE Is Prevalence-Sensitive for Conformal Scores}
A further protocol warning emerged from prevalence stress tests: ECE computed on $1-p$ conformal scores changes drastically with the anomaly prevalence of the evaluation set even when the ranking is fixed. On full VisA, LOIO ECE moves from 0.404 at 1\% prevalence to 0.149 at 50\%, and the LOIO-versus-weighted ranking reverses across this range. ECE on anomaly-balanced test sets therefore cannot certify deployment reliability of conformal-derived scores. We report ECE as a secondary metric only and rely on false-alarm rate, power, precision, and risk--coverage for operational claims. The risk--coverage view also quantifies the value of abstention: ranking images by predictive entropy and abstaining on the most uncertain 20\% halves the remaining LOIO calibration error on full MVTec (0.068 to 0.030) and improves it moderately on VisA (0.077 to 0.075 at 80\% coverage, 0.066 at 70\%), so selective operation is a practical complement to fixed-$\alpha$ alarms.

\subsection{Routing, Pixel Localization, and Fragility Diagnostics}
\input{tables/tab_protocol_routing}
Table~\ref{tab:protocol-routing} evaluates protocol-locked representative routing. Fixed LOIO conformal reduces ECE relative to Vector Platt without using validation labels to select an expert. This supports the central CRR claim: reliability routing can be useful even when the route is fixed and conservative. SAGE-style gating remains a promising design inspiration, but the current main evidence favors simple LOIO routing over learned or weighted expert selection.

\input{tables/tab_pixel_and_diagnostics}
Pixel-level results show that the low-storage subspace detector is also competitive for localization. Robustness diagnostics are less favorable: FGSM experiments show severe fragility and objective sensitivity. These results are important negative evidence and motivate reliability diagnostics, but they do not support an adversarial robustness claim.
