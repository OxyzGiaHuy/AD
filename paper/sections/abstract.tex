\begin{abstract}
Few-shot industrial anomaly detection increasingly uses frozen vision foundation features, especially DINOv2 patch embeddings with memory-bank or PCA/subspace scoring. These detectors can rank defects well, but their raw distances are not calibrated probabilities and often become unreliable under low-shot, dataset, class, or corruption shift. We present \emph{Conformal Reliability Routing} (CRR), a decoupled reliability framework for low-storage DINOv2 subspace anomaly detection. CRR keeps PCA/subspace residuals as the raw anomaly ranking score, while a separate reliability layer maps residual evidence to probability-like decision signals through vector calibration and leave-one-image-out (LOIO) conformal p-values computed only from normal support images. This design explicitly separates ``which sample is more anomalous'' from ``how reliable is the alarm probability.'' On MVTec AD, the resulting detector is competitive but not state-of-the-art in image AUROC, while requiring substantially less reference storage than memory-bank baselines. On VisA, a PCA128 variant improves AUROC over PCA64 by 0.011--0.017 with storage still below 0.6 MB. On full corruption benchmarks over all 12 VisA and all 15 MVTec classes with $k\in\{4,8\}$ and four corruptions, LOIO conformal reliability achieves overall ECE of 0.0766 and 0.0684 respectively and improves over Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt in every tested corruption cell on both datasets (paired Wilcoxon $p\le5\times10^{-9}$ except versus Shift-Aware Platt at VisA $k{=}8$; weighted conformal is a separate ablation, competitive only for selected $k{=}8$ structured corruptions). Beyond calibration error, we analyze conformal alarms operationally: an attainable-alpha analysis shows that $k$-shot conformal p-values cannot alarm below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$, and at the first attainable operating point the target-only rule is conservative on VisA but anti-conservative on corrupted MVTec. To obtain controlled alarms below and at this floor, we introduce SC3R, a source-validated cross-category thresholding scheme that pools normal images from other categories under matched conditions. Against a pre-registered decision gate on all 15 MVTec classes, SC3R tracks nominal false-alarm rates almost exactly (0.050/0.105/0.216 at $\alpha{=}0.05/0.10/0.20$) with detection power 0.22--0.42 where target-only alarms are structurally silent, and hierarchical class--seed bootstrap intervals for the power gain exclude zero for every corruption condition. The paper positions CRR as a calibrated, low-storage reliability layer for few-shot inspection rather than an MVTec AUROC SOTA or adversarial-robustness claim.
\end{abstract}
