\begin{abstract}
Few-shot industrial anomaly detection increasingly uses frozen vision foundation features, especially DINOv2 patch embeddings with memory-bank or PCA/subspace scoring. These detectors can rank defects well, but their raw distances are not calibrated probabilities and often become unreliable under low-shot, dataset, class, or corruption shift. We present \emph{Conformal Reliability Routing} (CRR), a decoupled reliability framework for low-storage DINOv2 subspace anomaly detection. CRR keeps PCA/subspace residuals as the raw anomaly ranking score, while a separate reliability layer maps residual evidence to probability-like decision signals through vector calibration and leave-one-image-out (LOIO) conformal p-values computed only from normal support images. This design explicitly separates ``which sample is more anomalous'' from ``how reliable is the alarm probability.'' On MVTec AD, the resulting detector is competitive but not state-of-the-art in image AUROC, while requiring substantially less reference storage than memory-bank baselines. On VisA, a PCA128 variant improves AUROC over PCA64 by 0.011--0.017 with storage still below 0.6 MB. On the full VisA corruption benchmark with 12 classes, $k\in\{4,8\}$, five seeds, and four corruptions, LOIO conformal reliability achieves an overall ECE of 0.0766 and improves over Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt in every tested corruption cell. The paper positions CRR as a calibrated, low-storage reliability layer for few-shot inspection rather than an MVTec AUROC SOTA or adversarial-robustness claim.
\end{abstract}
