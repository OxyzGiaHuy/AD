\begin{abstract}
Few-shot industrial anomaly detection has benefited from frozen vision foundation models, particularly DINOv2 patch features combined with memory-bank or subspace ranking. However, strong ranking does not imply reliable probabilities under low-shot, class, dataset, or corruption shift. We study a decoupled reliability layer for low-storage DINOv2 subspace anomaly detection: PCA residuals are preserved as the raw anomaly ranking, while leave-one-image-out conformal p-value views provide calibrated reliability estimates and risk signals. On a full VisA corruption benchmark with 12 classes, $k\in\{4,8\}$, five seeds, and four corruptions, the LOIO conformal view achieves an overall ECE of 0.0766 and improves over Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt in every tested corruption cell. The method does not claim MVTec AUROC SOTA or adversarial robustness; instead, it targets reliable decision support for low-storage few-shot inspection.
\end{abstract}
