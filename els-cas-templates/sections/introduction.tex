\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Industrial anomaly detection (AD) is commonly implemented in asymmetric data regimes, where normal examples are available, while abnormal examples are rare, heterogeneous, and expensive to annotate. Consequently, few-shot AD has become practically significant. In this context, an inspection system typically receives only $k$ normal support images for a target category, often with $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, and is required to detect both image-level and pixel-level defects during testing.

Recent approaches have improved performance in this regime by leveraging frozen visual foundation models. For example, DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} patch features can be stored in a normal memory bank and queried using nearest-neighbor distances, as implemented in PatchCore-style methods and AnomalyDINO~\cite{roth2022towards,damm2025anomalydino}. Alternatively, frozen patch features may be compressed into a normal principal component analysis (PCA) or subspace model, as in SubspaceAD~\cite{subspacead2026}. These methods enable effective anomaly ranking without the need to train a large task-specific backbone.

However, effective ranking does not equate to reliable decision support. A raw nearest-neighbor distance or PCA residual may yield high area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) while remaining poorly calibrated: a score threshold that is effective for one class may not correspond to the same false-alarm rate for another class, dataset, or under data corruption. This distinction is critical in industrial deployment, where operators require not only identification of suspicious samples but also trustworthy alarm probabilities. Previous studies have demonstrated that DINOv2-based few-shot AD can exhibit poor calibration and adversarial fragility~\cite{khan2025calibration}. As such, reliability is an integral aspect of the deployment challenge, rather than a superficial post-processing concern.

This work addresses the identified gap by decoupling anomaly ranking from reliability estimation. The central questions pertain to the learning system itself: what false-alarm behavior can a decision rule, calibrated on $k$ examples of a frozen representation, guarantee, at what resolution, and how does this guarantee degrade under input distribution shifts? The ranking component utilizes a frozen DINOv2 PCA/subspace detector, aggregating patch residuals into a raw anomaly score for AUROC, average precision (AP), and localization. A distinct reliability component transforms this evidence into calibrated or probability-like outputs. The primary target-only approach employs leave-one-image-out (LOIO) conformal calibration on the limited normal support images. Each support image is evaluated as if held out, generating normal nonconformity values; a test image subsequently receives a conformal p-value and a reliability score of $1-p$. This framework does not constitute a supervised anomaly posterior, but it is statistically interpretable and constructed without target anomaly labels.

The foundational components utilized in this study—DINOv2 features, PCA residuals, Platt-family calibration, and conformal anomaly detection—are well-established in the literature; the present contribution builds upon these elements. However, existing research leaves unresolved structural questions specific to the few-shot regime: for example, a conformal alarm calibrated on $k$ support images cannot trigger below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$, corruption shift can render the observed false-alarm rate anti-conservative, and a statistically valid source-category certificate may be vacuous when only a limited number of categories are available. The primary contribution integrates an exact resolution-floor diagnosis, an empirical audit under distribution shift, and \textbf{C}ross-category \textbf{R}eliability \textbf{E}stimation with \textbf{S}ource \textbf{S}upport (CRESS), a source-assisted thresholding and certification procedure evaluated using a strict nested protocol. The rigorous experimental protocol does not support the anticipated new-category claim: all category-certified thresholds revert to zero. This negative outcome is consistent with both the stated Hoeffding rule and a broader all-zero lower bound for deterministic, distribution-free category-risk confidence bounds, and is reported alongside, rather than in place of, fixed-archive image sensitivity and historical empirical results.

The implications for learning systems are broadly applicable, despite the industrial testbed: finite calibration constrains both the resolution of target-only alarms and the feasibility of source-category certification. This paper presents four contributions, with the first two being central:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We make the few-shot resolution limit of conformal alarms explicit through an attainable-alpha analysis: with $k$ supports, no alarm can trigger below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$, and empirical false-alarm rates must be interpreted relative to the attainable grid rather than the nominal level. Cluster-aware Monte Carlo diagnostics of normal p-values then localize when and in which direction the exchangeable-null pattern fails under corruption shift, being conservative on VisA and anti-conservative on corrupted MVTec.
    \item We formulate CRESS with disjoint reference, proposal, and certification categories and evaluate it under a strict nested protocol. In addition to the Hoeffding-specific feasibility condition, we prove that any deterministic uniformly valid distribution-free upper bound based solely on independent and identically distributed (iid) category losses has an all-zero floor $1-\beta^{1/n}$; even without multiplicity, 95\% certification at $\alpha=0.20$ requires 14 categories. The frozen experiment spans four $k$, five random seeds, five conditions, MVTec, VisA, and MVTec-to-VisA/MPDD transfer. Category certification yields a zero threshold in all 960 gate cells because only three or four certification categories are available; image-level fixed-archive sensitivity is reported separately and never relabeled as a new-category result.
    \item We formulate a decoupled pipeline: frozen DINOv2 PCA residuals are used for ranking, while a label-free layer provides Vector/Shift-Aware calibration and LOIO p-values~\cite{hennhofer2024loio} based on the normal supports. Across the historical MVTec and VisA corruption studies, LOIO reduces expected calibration error (ECE) compared to most tested calibrators without altering the raw ranking; operational metrics, rather than ECE, carry the central interpretation.
    \item We report accuracy-storage, transfer calibration, pixel-level, robustness, prevalence-stress, and routing diagnostics, including negative findings that prevent overclaiming. The framework is not MVTec AUROC SOTA, nor is it adversarially robust; ECE on conformal-derived scores is strongly prevalence-sensitive, and the historical CRESS gate is not an independent certificate.
\end{itemize}
