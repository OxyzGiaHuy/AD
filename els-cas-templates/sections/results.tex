\subsection{Ranking Substrate and Storage}
Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} presents clean image-level ranking performance and category-specific storage. The official AnomalyDINO reproduction (released code, three seeds) achieves the highest image AUROC at each reported $k$ on both datasets: 0.965, 0.976, and 0.980 on MVTec, and 0.857, 0.912, and 0.926 on VisA. Because these results follow the released method's own evaluation protocol, they are external accuracy references rather than direct evidence of superiority under our controlled protocol. WinCLIP is likewise a cited reference: its paper reports AUROC and AUPR at 1/4 shots, but not an AP implementation verified to match ours. The reported AUPR values are 0.965/0.973 on MVTec and 0.851/0.888 on VisA; we therefore mark its AP entries N/C instead of mixing them into the AP comparison. WinCLIP does not report ECE under our calibration protocol, category-specific storage under our accounting, or an 8-shot result. The absence of an official implementation and the community-reimplementation discrepancy are documented in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

Compared with the controlled DINOv2 nearest-neighbor ranker, the PCA64 residual ranker yields lower AUROC by 0.010/0.005/0.003 on MVTec and higher AUROC by 0.019/0.008/0.007 on VisA at $k=1/4/8$, respectively, while requiring 0.472\,MiB of category-specific state instead of 2.005-6.000\,MiB. Accordingly, PCA64 is treated as a compact supporting ranker rather than a ranking contribution or state-of-the-art result. On VisA, PCA128 increases class-averaged AUROC over PCA64 by 0.011/0.015/0.017 at $k=1/4/8$, with storage rising to 0.566\,MiB; this is a descriptive accuracy-storage ablation rather than a new ranking-method claim. The controlled nearest-neighbor rows are not official PatchCore or AnomalyDINO reproductions. Boldface in Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} marks the best result only among methods evaluated under our common protocol, external AnomalyDINO and WinCLIP references are excluded from this comparison.

The controlled nearest-neighbor ECE values diagnose a failed scalar calibration fit rather than a failure in ranking. Under the label-free scalar-Platt protocol, exact support-patch self-matches at low $k$ and the 4096-patch subsampling regime at larger $k$ coincide with near-constant test probabilities. On MVTec, ECE is 0.277 at $k=1$ and 0.693 at $k=4,8$, which correspond to the class-averaged prevalence complements; the Brier score is similarly close to ECE. These degenerate probabilities are not used as a competitive calibration baseline.

\emph{Storage and compute accounting.} Storage is reported in MiB and includes only category-specific state, excluding the shared frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone ($\approx22$M parameters, $\approx84\,\mathrm{MiB}$ in fp32), which is stored once per deployment for all compared frozen-feature methods. The 0.472\,MiB PCA64-based reliability state comprises the PCA mean and 64-component basis ($\approx0.095\,\mathrm{MiB}$), the synthetic-anomaly MLP head, and its feature-normalization statistics. Expanding the basis from 64 to 128 components adds 0.094\,MiB. The fitted three-feature Vector Platt scaler requires only tens of bytes, which is below the displayed precision. The controlled memory bank stores up to 4096 fp32 vectors of dimension 384, totaling exactly 6.000\,MiB at maximum capacity. For cached features, raw PCA-residual scoring requires approximately 1.3\,ms per image, compared with 5 to 13\,ms for controlled nearest-neighbor scoring. These timings exclude shared DINOv2 feature extraction and reflect only category-specific raw-score computation. LOIO also requires a one-time setup of $k$ leave-one-out PCA fits per category, each using up to $(k-1)\times1369$ support patches.

The clean benchmark applies max pooling, whereas the conformal pipeline uses the top-1\% mean. The representative aggregation ablation in Table~\ref{tab:agg-ablation} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) demonstrates that both choices are within 0.01 to 0.02 AUROC of the best tested setting. These results indicate that pooling does not drive the ranking comparison, but it does not establish invariance outside the tested grid.

\subsection{Target-Only Resolution and False-Alarm Behavior}\label{sec:target-only-operation}

Calibration error does not address the operator's main concern: ``\textit{if an alarm fires at $p\le\alpha$, how often is a normal part flagged?}'' Table~\ref{tab:attainable-alpha} first isolates the structural resolution limit. With $k=4$, the smallest p-value is $1/(k+1)=0.2$, so nominal levels 0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 cannot raise an alarm on either dataset. With $k=8$, the floor decreases to $1/9\approx0.111$, but the same three tested levels remain below it. Their zero FAR and detection values are therefore resolution artifacts of the finite calibration set, not evidence of effective conservative control.

At nominal $\alpha=0.20$, the effective attainable threshold is 0.20 for $k=4$ but only $1/9$ for $k=8$, because no $k=8$ grid point lies between $1/9$ and 0.20. On VisA, the resulting aggregate FAR/detection pairs are 0.152/0.599 at $k=4$ and 0.111/0.537 at $k=8$; on MVTec they are 0.411/0.887 and 0.304/0.907, respectively. Thus the same nominal $\alpha$ can induce different effective p-value thresholds across $k$, and the observed FAR itself is not restricted to the p-value grid. The aggregate contrast already indicates dataset-dependent behavior: VisA remains below nominal, whereas MVTec exceeds it substantially.


Table~\ref{tab:false-alarm-control} resolves the $\alpha=0.20$ aggregate by corruption. For VisA, FAR remains below the nominal level across all four corruptions, ranging from 0.142 to 0.157 at $k=4$ and from 0.103 to 0.123 at $k=8$. These operating points maintain nonzero detection rates of 0.585 to 0.610 and 0.522 to 0.547, respectively, with precision values between 0.803 and 0.843 under the benchmark prevalence.

In contrast, MVTec exhibits less stable behavior. At $k=4$, FAR ranges from 0.306 to 0.463 across corruptions, despite detection rates of 0.879 to 0.890. Increasing $k$ to 8 reduces FAR in every corruption cell and increases observed precision, but does not consistently restore nominal behavior. Specifically, blur and brightness/contrast are close to $\alpha$ (0.194 and 0.188), while Gaussian noise and JPEG remain anti-conservative (0.355 and 0.307). Thus, crossing the resolution floor results in nonzero alarms but does not guarantee stable false-alarm behavior under corruption shift. Increasing $k$ mitigates, but does not eliminate, the observed mismatch. Precision is reported descriptively, as it depends on the anomaly prevalence in the evaluation set.

A relevant numerical implementation detail is that an fp32 representation of $1/5$ may be 0.20000000298. Therefore, exact comparison with 0.2 can inadvertently exclude all floor-level alarms. All reported thresholding procedures apply a small comparison tolerance.

\subsection{Strict CRESS Category Certification}\label{sec:cress-strict-results}
Table~\ref{tab:strict-nested-sc3r} summarizes the frozen strict nested audit. The primary category-level result is uniformly negative. The 960 gate configurations include four source–target jobs, four routing modes, four $k$ values, three $\alpha$ levels, and five conditions. Each configuration is evaluated across the declared target cells and five support seeds when computing its nonzero-threshold rate. None achieves the required 80\% rate, as every category-certified target cell receives $\tau=0$. The category block applies only four certification categories per cell for MVTec and both transfer directions, and three for VisA. The smallest observed candidate UCB is 0.950 on MVTec, 1.000 on VisA, 0.961 for MVTec-to-VisA, and 0.986 for MVTec-to-MPDD. Since all values exceed the largest tested $\alpha=0.20$, no positive candidate passes the category-risk gate.

This result does not indicate a GPU or optimization failure. Proposition~3 demonstrates that with $A=3$, $\delta=0.05$, and even $M=1$, a zero-loss Hoeffding gate needs at least 60 categories at $\alpha=0.20$. Proposition~4 further indicates that this outcome is not solely a consequence of Hoeffding looseness: even for one fixed candidate without a multiplicity penalty, any deterministic, uniformly valid, distribution-free 95\% upper bound based only on iid category losses needs at least 14 all-zero categories to reach 0.20; the frozen family allocation increases this requirement to 22. Both thresholds exceed the available three or four independent certification categories.

The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:strict-nested-sc3r} shifts the certification unit from categories to images within a fixed source archive. With 72 to 191 image units per cell, the smallest observed UCB decreases to 0.039 to 0.048, which is below at least one tested $\alpha$. Positive thresholds are observed in 36.7\% of MVTec, 60.4\% of VisA, 41.5\% of MVTec-to-VisA, and 37.0\% of MVTec-to-MPDD cells. However, none of these aggregate rates meet the 80\% operational criterion. More significantly, the increased image count tightens the bound for the fixed archive mixture but does not introduce independent categories or establish reliability for a new target category. Repeated corruption views are excluded as independent images. Thus, the image-level result serves as a sensitivity analysis rather than a resolution of the category-level failure or evidence of target-category false-alarm control.


\subsection{Conformal Reliability under Corruption}\label{sec:corruption-reliability}

Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal} reports the full VisA aggregate results using a fixed PCA/subspace ranking score. Across 56{,}000 corruption-view rows, LOIO conformal achieves a pooled ECE of 0.0766, with values of 0.0391 at $k=4$ and 0.1140 at $k=8$. Weighted conformal exhibits a substantially higher ECE at $k=4$ and converges toward LOIO at $k=8$ (0.116 versus 0.114). The near-tie in $k=8$ ECE does not extend to the other reported losses: LOIO has lower Brier score (0.188 versus 0.229) and NLL (0.612 versus 0.701). AUROC and AP remain identical between the two routes in every group because the underlying anomaly score is fixed; thus, the reliability transformation does not claim to improve ranking.

In the comprehensive MVTec study (15 classes, $k\in\{4,8\}$, seeds 0–2, four corruptions, 37{,}464 rows), LOIO achieves an overall ECE of 0.0684 (0.0600 at $k=4$ and 0.0769 at $k=8$) while maintaining an image AUROC of 0.912. LOIO consistently yields lower ECE than both Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt across all eight $k$–corruption settings; for instance, at MVTec $k=4$ under Gaussian noise, ECE is 0.033 compared to 0.233 and 0.270, respectively. This pattern is descriptive across the tested cells and does not represent a confirmatory significance result. ECE remains a secondary metric and does not guarantee control over false alarms.

Table~\ref{tab:visa-ece-corruption} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) presents VisA per-corruption values, and Figure~\ref{fig:ece-corruption} compares both datasets. LOIO has the lowest ECE in all four VisA $k=4$ cells and under Gaussian noise at $k=8$, whereas weighted conformal achieves lower ECE for the remaining three VisA $k=8$ corruptions. Across the MVTec panels, LOIO consistently exhibits the lowest ECE in every displayed cell. This cellwise summary provides a more accurate characterization than describing either route as uniformly superior.

Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} reveals the distributional patterns that scalar ECE summaries obscure. The attainable confidence values are sparse because the LOIO construction yields only $k+1$ p-value levels. In both datasets, the pooled $k=8$ curve lies further below the diagonal than the $k=4$ curve in the upper-confidence region, which aligns with the higher pooled LOIO ECE at $k=8$. As these diagrams aggregate across classes, seeds, and corruptions, they diagnose overall miscalibration but do not indicate that every category exhibits the same direction or magnitude of error.

\subsection{Distributional and Calibration Diagnostics}\label{sec:uniformity}
Conformal p-values satisfy $P(p\le\alpha)\le\alpha$ for normal data only when exchangeability conditions are met. This property reflects a false-alarm rate rather than a posterior probability. Because a $k$-shot LOIO p-value is confined to the grid $\{j/(k+1)\}$, the application of a continuous Kolmogorov–Smirnov test against $U(0,1)$ is inappropriate. Figure~\ref{fig:uniformity} instead compares the empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) of normal p-values to the ideal discrete-uniform reference at each grid point. Deviations are conservative for VisA at $k=4$ (with the largest negative gap of $-0.10$ at 0.4), whereas VisA at $k=8$ and MVTec at both $k$ values become anti-conservative under corruption. For instance, for MVTec $k=4$ with Gaussian noise, $\hat{F}(0.2)=0.46$ compared to the reference value of 0.20. Because rows share calibration sets, classes, and repeated seeds, a cluster-preserving exchangeable Monte Carlo diagnostic is employed rather than an independent and identically distributed (iid) confirmatory test. The results indicate that corrupted normal residuals increase relative to clean LOIO calibration scores, although a causal mechanism is not identified. A matched-LOIO audit on representative MVTec $k=4$ classes increases the clean FAR from 0.17 to 0.25, with power remaining essentially unchanged. Thus, fold asymmetry alone does not account for the observed behavior.

Table~\ref{tab:scalar-calibrators} presents comparisons across class, seed, and corruption for Vector Platt, Shift-Aware Platt, temperature scaling, isotonic regression, histogram binning, and scalar Platt, all evaluated under a consistent label-free protocol. In contrast to the pooled expected calibration error (ECE) in Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal}, these results first compute ECE within each class–seed–corruption cell and then report the mean and standard deviation across cells; as a result, the two summaries are not necessarily numerically equivalent. LOIO demonstrates lower mean per-cell ECE in most comparisons (for example, MVTec $k=4$: LOIO $0.120\pm0.054$ versus isotonic $0.222\pm0.088$, temperature $0.240\pm0.104$, and histogram binning $0.229\pm0.099$), although the margin decreases at $k=8$. LOIO achieves lower ECE than isotonic regression in 55\% of MVTec cells and 62\% of VisA cells. Against Shift-Aware Platt on VisA $k=8$, the mean difference is $-0.011$, with LOIO exhibiting lower ECE in 51\% of cells. Historical unadjusted significance values are not reported because a complete multiplicity-adjusted analysis is unavailable, and no confirmatory significance claims are made. Equal-mass ECE increases LOIO's per-cell error compared with equal-width ECE, underscoring the importance of the operational analyses discussed above.

ECE on $1-p$ is sensitive to anomaly prevalence, even when the ranking remains unchanged. On the full VisA dataset, LOIO ECE decreases from 0.404 at 1\% anomaly prevalence to 0.149 at 50\%, and the ordering between LOIO and weighted methods reverses across this range. Consequently, anomaly-balanced ECE does not guarantee deployment reliability. Throughout this paper, FAR, power, precision, and risk-coverage are used for operational interpretation.

\subsection{Historical, Non-Independent CRESS Sensitivity}\label{sec:cress-historical}
These analyses were performed before implementing the strict nested protocol and are presented exclusively as empirical sensitivity checks. As they reuse source evidence and employ pointwise rather than simultaneous intervals, these results do not supersede the category-level findings reported in Section~\ref{sec:cress-strict-results}.

Across all 15 label-stratified MVTec classes at $k=4$ (seeds 0–2, five conditions, 675 evaluation cells), matched-condition CRESS demonstrates mean power of 0.216 and 0.416 at $\alpha=0.05$ and 0.10, respectively, when the target-only anchor is inactive. The mean FAR is 0.050, 0.105, and 0.216 at $\alpha=0.05$, 0.10, and 0.20, respectively. Precision exceeds 0.90, and no-harm rates are 89\%, 82\%, and 89\%. Although the historical empirical gate generally passes, certain condition-level exceptions remain: JPEG FAR is 0.121 at $\alpha=0.10$, and Gaussian noise and JPEG reach 0.226 and 0.235 at $\alpha=0.20$, surpassing the $\alpha+0.02$ thresholds.

Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-source-validated} further illustrates the power and FAR trade-off when the target-only anchor is active. At $\alpha=0.20$, historical CRESS reduces FAR compared to the anchor in every condition by 0.058 to 0.234, but its power is also reduced by 0.094 to 0.365. The most substantial simultaneous reduction in FAR and power occurs under Gaussian noise. These paired changes indicate an empirical threshold-selection trade-off rather than superiority of CRESS over the anchor.

Supporting tables are available in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}. At $k=8$, matched-condition CRESS achieves pooled power of 0.547 and 0.693 at the two nominal levels, both below the target-only floor. The pooled FARs of 0.052, 0.121, and 0.230, as well as no-harm rates of 80\%, 74\%, and 81\%, meet the historical empirical budget only at $\alpha=0.05$ (Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-k8}). The breakdown by condition is less uniform than the pooled values. At $\alpha=0.10$, clean, blur, and brightness/contrast remain within the $\alpha+0.02$ budget, while Gaussian noise and JPEG reach FAR values of 0.138 and 0.141, respectively. At $\alpha=0.20$, only the clean condition remains within budget; blur is marginally above at 0.221, and brightness/contrast, Gaussian noise, and JPEG reach 0.223, 0.232, and 0.260, respectively. Despite these results, sub-floor precision remains high (0.936 to 0.976), indicating that the primary limitation is in false-alarm control rather than a lack of detected anomalies.

Smoothed randomized p-values enable alarms below the deterministic floor but maintain the target-only mismatch. On pooled MVTec at $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.10$, randomization yields FAR values of 0.085 and 0.170 and power of 0.216 and 0.431, respectively, whereas historical CRESS yields FAR values of 0.049 and 0.105 and power of 0.216 and 0.416 (Table~\ref{tab:randomized-pvalue}). On VisA, randomization is more conservative, with FAR values of 0.036 and 0.072 compared to 0.051 and 0.095. Its power is slightly higher at $\alpha=0.05$ (0.150 versus 0.144) but lower at $\alpha=0.10$ (0.300 versus 0.351). Therefore, neither approach consistently outperforms the other across datasets and operating levels. The historical CRESS comparison remains non-independent and cannot be considered certified evidence.

Within-VisA sources yield FAR values of 0.051, 0.095, and 0.192 and power of 0.144, 0.351, and 0.637, with precision of 0.847, 0.843, and 0.810 and no-harm rates of 89\%, 84\%, and 78\% (Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-visa}). Replacing these with MVTec sources reduces FAR to 0.013, 0.040, and 0.098 but also decreases power to 0.091, 0.201, and 0.415. Precision increases to 0.936, 0.881, and 0.852, and no-harm rates rise to 97\%, 93\%, and 93\%. The foreign archive is therefore more conservative in this transfer direction; however, the associated reduction in power precludes interpreting this as a general safety property.

\subsection{Secondary Deployment and Detector Diagnostics}
The risk-coverage analysis presented in Figure~\ref{fig:risk-coverage} ranks images according to predictive entropy. Abstaining from the most uncertain 20\% reduces LOIO ECE from 0.068 to 0.030 on the full MVTec dataset, but only from 0.077 to 0.075 on VisA. Notably, VisA achieves 0.066 at 70\% coverage. These results indicate that the benefit of abstention is dataset-dependent rather than a uniform outcome. This analysis serves as a selective-operation diagnostic rather than a formal certificate.

Table~\ref{tab:protocol-routing} evaluates protocol-locked representative routing. Employing fixed LOIO reduces ECE from 0.301–0.361 to 0.099–0.154 across both transfer directions, LOCO, and within-split evaluations. The corresponding $\Delta$ECE ranges from $-0.207$ to $-0.188$, with 100\% no-harm observed in the reported cells. These findings support using a simple fixed target-only route as the main baseline, without validation-label expert selection. Because the table covers representative locked protocols and ECE only, it does not demonstrate that LOIO outperforms learned or weighted routing across all datasets, shifts, or operational metrics.

Table~\ref{tab:pixel-diagnostics} distinguishes between localization and robustness. PCA64 surpasses the controlled memory bank in pixel AUROC by only 0.004, 0.006, and 0.008 at $k=1$, $k=4$, and $k=8$, respectively. AU-PRO reveals a complementary trade-off: PCA64 performs lower at $k=1$ (0.820 versus 0.845), matches at $k=4$ (0.862), and outperforms at $k=8$ (0.876 versus 0.863). Thus, the low-storage ranker remains competitive in this controlled localization setting, but the small, metric-dependent differences do not justify a broad claim of localization superiority.

The fragility block yields less favorable results. A label-aware PCA-residual FGSM surrogate reduces image AUROC from 0.937 and 0.945 to 0.158 and 0.175 at $\varepsilon=2/255$, which is below chance due to inverted score ordering. The degradation is non-monotonic; at $8/255$, AUROC recovers to 0.446 for both $k$ values. This pattern demonstrates sensitivity to the surrogate objective and attack configuration. These findings are diagnostic and do not substantiate an adversarial-robustness claim.
