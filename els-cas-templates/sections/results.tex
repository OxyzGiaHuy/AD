\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Ranking Substrate and Storage}
\input{tables/tab_clean_efficiency}

Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} presents clean image-level ranking performance and category-specific storage. The official AnomalyDINO reproduction achieves the highest image AUROC at each reported $k$ on both datasets, but follows its released evaluation protocol and therefore serves as an external accuracy reference rather than evidence of superiority under our controlled protocol. WinCLIP is likewise a cited reference: its paper reports AUROC and AUPR at one and four shots, but not an AP implementation verified to match ours. We therefore mark its AP entries N/C instead of mixing the reported AUPR values into the controlled AP comparison. WinCLIP also does not report ECE under our calibration protocol, category-specific storage under our accounting, or an eight-shot result.

Compared with the controlled DINOv2 nearest-neighbor ranker, PCA64 yields lower AUROC by 0.010, 0.005, and 0.003 on MVTec and higher AUROC by 0.019, 0.008, and 0.007 on VisA at $k=1,4,8$, respectively, while requiring 0.472\,MiB of category-specific state instead of 2.005 to 6.000\,MiB. PCA64 is therefore a compact supporting ranker, not a ranking contribution or state-of-the-art result. On VisA, PCA128 increases class-averaged AUROC over PCA64 by 0.011, 0.015, and 0.017, with storage rising to 0.566\,MiB. Boldface is restricted to methods evaluated under our common protocol; external AnomalyDINO and WinCLIP references are excluded.

The controlled nearest-neighbor ECE values diagnose a failed scalar calibration fit rather than a ranking failure. Exact support-patch self-matches at low $k$ and the 4096-patch subsampling regime at larger $k$ coincide with near-constant test probabilities. These degenerate probabilities are not used as a competitive calibration baseline.

\emph{Storage and compute accounting.} Storage excludes the shared frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone, which is stored once for all categories. The 0.472\,MiB PCA64-based reliability state comprises the PCA mean and 64-component basis, the synthetic-anomaly MLP head, and feature-normalization statistics. Expanding to 128 components adds 0.094\,MiB. The controlled memory bank stores up to 4096 fp32 vectors of dimension 384, or 6.000\,MiB. With cached features, PCA-residual scoring takes approximately 1.3\,ms per image, compared with 5 to 13\,ms for controlled nearest-neighbor scoring. These timings exclude shared backbone inference. CRESS subsequently retains scalar normalized reference scores and partition metadata rather than source patch features.

The clean benchmark uses max pooling, whereas the conformal pipeline uses the top-$1\%$ mean. Table~\ref{tab:agg-ablation} in Appendix~\ref{app:tables} shows that both choices lie within 0.01 to 0.02 AUROC of the best tested setting. Thus pooling does not drive the reported ranking comparison, although invariance outside the tested grid is not claimed.

\subsection{Target-Only Resolution and False-Alarm Behavior}\label{sec:target-only-operation}
\input{tables/tab_attainable_alpha}
\input{tables/tab_false_alarm_control}

Table~\ref{tab:attainable-alpha} isolates the structural resolution limit. With $k=4$, the smallest p-value is $1/(k+1)=0.2$, so nominal levels 0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 cannot raise an alarm. With $k=8$, the floor decreases to $1/9\approx0.111$, but the same three levels remain below it. Their zero FAR and detection values are therefore finite-resolution artifacts, not evidence of effective conservative control.

At nominal $\alpha=0.20$, the effective threshold is 0.20 for $k=4$ but only $1/9$ for $k=8$, because $1/9$ is the largest $k=8$ grid point not exceeding 0.20. On VisA, the aggregate FAR/detection pairs are 0.152/0.599 at $k=4$ and 0.111/0.537 at $k=8$; on MVTec they are 0.373/0.886 and 0.261/0.907. These values first pool images over categories and seeds within each corruption and then average the four corruption-specific rates. The same nominal $\alpha$ can therefore induce different effective thresholds across $k$, while the observed FAR itself is not restricted to the p-value grid.

Table~\ref{tab:false-alarm-control} resolves this aggregate by corruption. VisA remains below the nominal level in all cells, with FAR from 0.142 to 0.157 at $k=4$ and from 0.103 to 0.123 at $k=8$. MVTec is less stable: at $k=4$, FAR ranges from 0.306 to 0.463; at $k=8$, blur and brightness/contrast approach $\alpha$ at 0.194 and 0.188, while Gaussian noise and JPEG remain anti-conservative at 0.355 and 0.307. Increasing $k$ mitigates but does not eliminate the mismatch. Precision is descriptive because it depends on benchmark anomaly prevalence.

An fp32 representation of $1/5$ may equal 0.20000000298, so exact comparison with 0.2 can inadvertently exclude floor-level alarms. All reported thresholding applies a fixed numerical tolerance.

\subsection{Normal-P-Value Shift Audit}\label{sec:uniformity}
Conformal p-values satisfy $P(p\leq\alpha)\leq\alpha$ for normal data only under the relevant exchangeability conditions. Because a $k$-score p-value lies on the grid $\{j/(k+1)\}$, a continuous Kolmogorov--Smirnov test against $U(0,1)$ is inappropriate. Figure~\ref{fig:uniformity} instead compares the empirical normal-p-value CDF with the ideal discrete-uniform reference at each attainable point. VisA at $k=4$ is conservative, with the largest negative gap of $-0.10$ at 0.4, whereas VisA at $k=8$ and MVTec at both $k$ values become anti-conservative under corruption. For example, MVTec $k=4$ under Gaussian noise has $\widehat F(0.2)=0.46$ compared with the reference value 0.20.

Rows share calibration sets, categories, and repeated seeds, so the audit uses a cluster-preserving exchangeable Monte Carlo diagnostic rather than an iid confirmatory test. The results indicate that corrupted normal residuals increase relative to clean support calibration scores, but do not identify a causal mechanism. A matched-LOIO audit on representative MVTec $k=4$ categories increases clean FAR from 0.17 to 0.25 while leaving power essentially unchanged. Fold asymmetry therefore does not account for the observed anti-conservative behavior.

\subsection{Strict CRESS Category Certification}\label{sec:cress-strict-results}
\input{tables/tab_strict_nested_sc3r}
\input{tables/tab_pooled_source_conformal}

Table~\ref{tab:strict-nested-sc3r} summarizes the frozen strict nested audit. The primary category-level result is uniformly negative. The 960 gate configurations comprise four source-to-target jobs, four source-view modes, four $k$ values, three $\alpha$ levels, and five conditions. None reaches the required 80\% nonzero-threshold rate: every category-certified target cell receives $\tau^\star=0$. Only four certification categories are available per MVTec and transfer cell and three per VisA cell. The smallest candidate UCB is 0.950 on MVTec, 1.000 on VisA, 0.961 for MVTec-to-VisA, and 0.986 for MVTec-to-MPDD, all exceeding the largest tested $\alpha=0.20$.

This outcome is not a GPU or optimization failure. Proposition~3 shows that with $A=3$, $\delta=0.05$, and even $M=1$, a zero-loss Hoeffding gate needs at least 60 categories at $\alpha=0.20$. Proposition~4 shows that the finding is not solely due to Hoeffding looseness: without multiplicity, any deterministic uniformly valid distribution-free 95\% upper bound based only on iid category losses needs at least 14 all-zero categories to reach 0.20; the frozen family allocation increases this requirement to 22. Both exceed the available three or four categories.

The image-unit block changes the estimand to a fixed source archive. With 72 to 191 image units per cell, the smallest UCB decreases to 0.039 to 0.048. Positive thresholds occur in 36.7\% of MVTec, 60.4\% of VisA, 41.5\% of MVTec-to-VisA, and 37.0\% of MVTec-to-MPDD cells. These rates do not meet the 80\% operational criterion, and the additional images do not create independent categories. The image result is therefore a fixed-archive sensitivity analysis, not a new-category certificate.

Table~\ref{tab:pooled-source-conformal} reports direct pooled-source conformal as an uncertified reference. It uses the complete selected source pool with $\tau=\alpha$ and no disjoint reference, proposal, or certification categories. Under matched-condition source selection, it has nonzero power in all four source-to-target jobs, but mean FAR exceeds nominal within both MVTec and VisA. Transfer is more conservative but also less powerful. Direct pooling therefore supplies finer operating points than target-only LOIO, while characterizing empirical risk under the selected source mixture rather than certified new-category risk.

\subsection{Secondary Calibration Diagnostics}\label{sec:corruption-reliability}
\input{tables/tab_visa_full_conformal}

Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal} reports the full VisA calibration summaries under a fixed ranking score. Across 56{,}000 corruption-view rows, LOIO has pooled ECE 0.0766, with 0.0391 at $k=4$ and 0.1140 at $k=8$. Weighted conformal is substantially worse at $k=4$ and approaches LOIO at $k=8$, but has higher Brier score and NLL. AUROC and AP remain identical because neither reliability transformation changes $s_c(x)$.

On the corresponding MVTec study, LOIO has overall ECE 0.0684 while maintaining image AUROC 0.912. LOIO is lower than Vector and Shift-Aware Platt in all eight displayed $k$-by-corruption cells, but this is a descriptive pattern rather than a multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory result. ECE remains secondary and does not imply false-alarm control.

\subsection{Historical, Non-Independent CRESS Sensitivity}\label{sec:cress-historical}
The historical analyses reuse source evidence and employ pointwise rather than simultaneous intervals. They are retained in the Appendix as empirical sensitivity checks and do not supersede the strict category result.

On MVTec $k=4$, historical matched-condition CRESS has pooled power 0.216 and 0.416 at $\alpha=0.05$ and 0.10, with FAR 0.050 and 0.105. At $k=8$, pooled power is 0.547 and 0.693, while pooled FAR is 0.052 and 0.121. Condition-level exceptions, particularly Gaussian noise and JPEG, prevent a uniform control claim. The paired changes in FAR and power in Tables~\ref{tab:sc3r-source-validated} and~\ref{tab:sc3r-k8} indicate a threshold-selection trade-off rather than superiority over the target-only anchor.

Smoothed randomized p-values and historical CRESS each enable sub-floor alarms, but neither dominates across datasets and operating levels (Table~\ref{tab:randomized-pvalue}). Within-VisA and MVTec-to-VisA source pools likewise display different FAR and power trade-offs (Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-visa}). Because threshold selection and source assessment are not independent in these analyses, none is interpreted as certified evidence.

Table~\ref{tab:pixel-diagnostics} distinguishes localization from robustness. PCA64 remains competitive with the controlled memory bank in pixel AUROC and AU-PRO, but the small metric-dependent differences do not support a general localization-superiority claim. These results diagnose sensitivity to the surrogate objective and attack configuration; they do not support adversarial robustness.
