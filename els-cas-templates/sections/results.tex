\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Ranking Substrate and Storage}
\input{tables/tab_clean_efficiency}
Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} presents clean image-level ranking performance and category-specific storage. The official AnomalyDINO reproduction (released code, three seeds) achieves the highest image AUROC at each reported $k$ on both datasets: 0.965, 0.976, and 0.980 on MVTec, and 0.857, 0.912, and 0.926 on VisA. Because these results follow the released method's own evaluation protocol, they are external accuracy references rather than direct evidence of superiority under our controlled protocol. WinCLIP is likewise a cited reference: its paper reports AUROC and AUPR at 1/4 shots, but not an AP implementation verified to match ours. The reported AUPR values are 0.965/0.973 on MVTec and 0.851/0.888 on VisA; we therefore mark its AP entries N/C instead of mixing them into the AP comparison. WinCLIP does not report ECE under our calibration protocol, category-specific storage under our accounting, or an 8-shot result. The absence of an official implementation and the community-reimplementation discrepancy are documented in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

Compared with the controlled DINOv2 nearest-neighbor ranker, the PCA64 residual ranker yields lower AUROC by 0.010/0.005/0.003 on MVTec and higher AUROC by 0.019/0.008/0.007 on VisA at $k=1/4/8$, respectively, while requiring 0.472\,MiB of category-specific state instead of 2.005--6.000\,MiB. Accordingly, PCA64 is treated as a compact supporting ranker rather than a ranking contribution or state-of-the-art result. On VisA, PCA128 increases class-averaged AUROC over PCA64 by 0.011/0.015/0.017 at $k=1/4/8$, with storage rising to 0.566\,MiB; this is a descriptive accuracy--storage ablation rather than a new ranking-method claim. The controlled nearest-neighbor rows are not official PatchCore or AnomalyDINO reproductions. Boldface in Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} is therefore restricted to comparisons among controlled-protocol rows.

The controlled nearest-neighbor ECE values diagnose a failed scalar calibration fit rather than a failure in ranking. Under the label-free scalar-Platt protocol, exact support-patch self-matches at low $k$ and the 4096-patch subsampling regime at larger $k$ coincide with near-constant test probabilities. On MVTec, ECE is 0.277 at $k=1$ and 0.693 at $k=4,8$, which correspond to the class-averaged prevalence complements; the Brier score is similarly close to ECE. These degenerate probabilities are not used as a competitive calibration baseline.

\emph{Storage and compute accounting.} Storage is reported in MiB and includes only category-specific state, excluding the shared frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone ($\approx22$M parameters, $\approx84\,\mathrm{MiB}$ in fp32), which is stored once per deployment for all compared frozen-feature methods. The 0.472\,MiB PCA64-based reliability state consists of the PCA mean and 64-component basis ($\approx0.095\,\mathrm{MiB}$), the synthetic-anomaly MLP head, and its feature-normalization statistics. Increasing the basis from 64 to 128 components adds 0.094\,MiB. The fitted three-feature Vector Platt scaler requires only tens of bytes, which is below the displayed precision. The controlled memory bank stores up to 4096 fp32 vectors of dimension 384, exactly 6.000\,MiB at the limit. For cached features, raw PCA-residual scoring requires approximately 1.3\,ms per image, compared with 5--13\,ms for controlled nearest-neighbor scoring. These timings exclude shared DINOv2 feature extraction and reflect only category-specific raw-score computation. LOIO also requires a one-time setup of $k$ leave-one-out PCA fits per category, each using up to $(k-1)\times1369$ support patches.

The clean benchmark uses max pooling, while the conformal pipeline uses the top-1\% mean. The representative aggregation ablation in Table~\ref{tab:agg-ablation} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) shows that both choices are within 0.01 to 0.02 AUROC of the best tested setting. This suggests that pooling does not drive the ranking comparison, but it does not establish invariance outside the tested grid.

\subsection{Target-Only Resolution and False-Alarm Behavior}\label{sec:target-only-operation}
\input{tables/tab_attainable_alpha}
Calibration error does not address the operator's main concern: ``if an alarm fires at $p\le\alpha$, how often is a normal part flagged?'' Table~\ref{tab:attainable-alpha} first isolates the structural resolution limit. With $k=4$, the smallest p-value is $1/(k+1)=0.2$, so nominal levels 0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 cannot raise an alarm on either dataset. With $k=8$, the floor decreases to $1/9\approx0.111$, but the same three tested levels remain below it. Their zero FAR and detection values are therefore resolution artifacts of the finite calibration set, not evidence of effective conservative control.

At nominal $\alpha=0.20$, the effective attainable threshold is 0.20 for $k=4$ but only $1/9$ for $k=8$, because no $k=8$ grid point lies between $1/9$ and 0.20. On VisA, the resulting aggregate FAR/detection pairs are 0.152/0.599 at $k=4$ and 0.111/0.537 at $k=8$; on MVTec they are 0.411/0.887 and 0.304/0.907, respectively. Thus the same nominal $\alpha$ can induce different effective p-value thresholds across $k$, and the observed FAR itself is not restricted to the p-value grid. The aggregate contrast already indicates dataset-dependent behavior: VisA remains below nominal, whereas MVTec exceeds it substantially.

\input{tables/tab_false_alarm_control}
Table~\ref{tab:false-alarm-control} resolves the $\alpha=0.20$ aggregate by corruption. On VisA, FAR remains below nominal across all four corruptions: 0.142--0.157 at $k=4$ and 0.103--0.123 at $k=8$. These operating points retain nonzero detection rates of 0.585--0.610 and 0.522--0.547, respectively, with precision between 0.803 and 0.843 under the benchmark prevalence.

The corresponding MVTec behavior is less stable. At $k=4$, FAR ranges from 0.306 to 0.463 across corruptions despite detection rates of 0.879--0.890. Increasing to $k=8$ lowers FAR in every corruption cell and raises the observed precision, but does not uniformly restore nominal behavior: blur and brightness/contrast are near $\alpha$ (0.194 and 0.188), whereas Gaussian noise and JPEG remain anti-conservative (0.355 and 0.307). Crossing the resolution floor therefore produces nonzero alarms but does not by itself ensure stable false-alarm behavior under corruption shift; increasing $k$ mitigates, rather than eliminates, the observed mismatch. Precision is reported descriptively because it depends on the anomaly prevalence of the evaluation set.

A numerical implementation detail is also relevant: an fp32 representation of $1/5$ may be 0.20000000298, so exact comparison with 0.2 can inadvertently exclude every floor-level alarm. All reported thresholding applies a small comparison tolerance.

\subsection{Strict CRESS Category Certification}\label{sec:cress-strict-results}
\input{tables/tab_strict_nested_sc3r}
Table~\ref{tab:strict-nested-sc3r} summarizes the frozen strict nested audit. The primary category-level result is uniformly negative. The 960 gate configurations comprise four source--target jobs, four routing modes, four $k$ values, three $\alpha$ levels, and five conditions; each configuration is evaluated over the declared target cells and five support seeds when computing its nonzero-threshold rate. None reaches the required 80\% rate, because every category-certified target cell receives $\tau=0$. The category block uses only four certification categories per cell for MVTec and both transfer directions, and three for VisA. Its smallest observed candidate UCB is 0.950 on MVTec, 1.000 on VisA, 0.961 for MVTec-to-VisA, and 0.986 for MVTec-to-MPDD. Because every value exceeds the largest tested $\alpha=0.20$, no positive candidate can pass the category-risk gate.

This outcome is not a GPU or optimization failure. Proposition~3 demonstrates that with $A=3$, $\delta=0.05$, and even $M=1$, a zero-loss Hoeffding gate needs at least 60 categories at $\alpha=0.20$. Proposition~4 further indicates that this result is not solely a consequence of Hoeffding looseness: even for one fixed candidate without a multiplicity penalty, any deterministic, uniformly valid, distribution-free 95\% upper bound based only on iid category losses needs at least 14 all-zero categories to reach 0.20; the frozen family allocation increases this to 22. Both requirements exceed the available three or four independent certification categories.

The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:strict-nested-sc3r} changes the certification unit from categories to images in a fixed source archive. With 72--191 image units per cell, the smallest observed UCB decreases to 0.039--0.048, below at least one tested $\alpha$, and positive thresholds occur in 36.7\% of MVTec, 60.4\% of VisA, 41.5\% of MVTec-to-VisA, and 37.0\% of MVTec-to-MPDD cells. None of these aggregate rates reaches the 80\% operational criterion. More importantly, the larger image count tightens a bound for the fixed archive mixture; it does not add independent categories or establish reliability for a new target category. Repeated corruption views are not counted as independent images. The image-level result is therefore a sensitivity analysis, not a resolution of the category-level failure or evidence of target-category false-alarm control.

\subsection{Conformal Reliability under Corruption}\label{sec:corruption-reliability}
\input{tables/tab_visa_full_conformal}
Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal} presents the full VisA aggregate using a fixed PCA/subspace ranking score. Across 56{,}000 corruption-view rows, LOIO conformal attains a pooled ECE of 0.0766, with 0.0391 at $k=4$ and 0.1140 at $k=8$. Weighted conformal has substantially higher ECE at $k=4$ and approaches LOIO at $k=8$ (0.116 versus 0.114). The near-tie in $k=8$ ECE does not extend to the other reported losses: LOIO has lower Brier score (0.188 versus 0.229) and NLL (0.612 versus 0.701). AUROC and AP are identical between the two routes in every group because the underlying anomaly score is fixed; the reliability transformation therefore makes no ranking-improvement claim.

In the full MVTec study (15 classes, $k\in\{4,8\}$, seeds 0--2, four corruptions, 37{,}464 rows), LOIO attains an overall ECE of 0.0684 (0.0600 at $k=4$ and 0.0769 at $k=8$) while maintaining an image AUROC of 0.912. LOIO has lower ECE than Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt in all eight $k$--corruption settings; for example, at MVTec $k=4$ under Gaussian noise, ECE is 0.033 compared with 0.233 and 0.270. This is a consistent descriptive pattern over the tested cells, not a confirmatory significance result. ECE remains a secondary metric and does not provide a false-alarm guarantee.

Table~\ref{tab:visa-ece-corruption} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) provides VisA per-corruption values, and Figure~\ref{fig:ece-corruption} compares both datasets. LOIO has the lowest ECE in all four VisA $k=4$ cells and under Gaussian noise at $k=8$; weighted conformal is lower for the other three VisA $k=8$ corruptions. Across the MVTec panels, LOIO is the lowest-ECE route in every displayed cell. This cellwise summary is more precise than describing either route as uniformly best.

Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} exposes the shape hidden by the scalar ECE summaries. The attainable confidence values are sparse because the LOIO construction has only $k+1$ p-value levels. In both datasets, the pooled $k=8$ curve lies farther below the diagonal than the $k=4$ curve over the upper-confidence region, consistent with the higher pooled LOIO ECE at $k=8$. Because these diagrams pool classes, seeds, and corruptions, they diagnose aggregate miscalibration but do not establish that every category has the same direction or magnitude of error.

\subsection{Distributional and Calibration Diagnostics}\label{sec:uniformity}
Conformal p-values satisfy $P(p\le\alpha)\le\alpha$ for normal data only when exchangeability conditions hold. This represents a false-alarm rate, not a posterior probability. Since a $k$-shot LOIO p-value is restricted to the grid $\{j/(k+1)\}$, applying a continuous Kolmogorov--Smirnov test against $U(0,1)$ is not valid. Figure~\ref{fig:uniformity} instead compares the empirical CDF of normal p-values to the ideal discrete-uniform reference at each grid point. Deviations are conservative for VisA at $k=4$ (worst gap $-0.10$ at 0.4), but VisA at $k=8$ and MVTec at both $k$ become anti-conservative under corruption. For example, for MVTec $k=4$ with Gaussian noise, $\hat{F}(0.2)=0.46$ compared to the reference 0.20. Because rows share calibration sets, classes, and repeated seeds, we use a cluster-preserving exchangeable Monte Carlo diagnostic rather than an iid confirmatory test. The results suggest that corrupted normal residuals increase relative to clean LOIO calibration scores, but do not identify a causal mechanism. A matched-LOIO audit on representative MVTec $k=4$ classes increases clean FAR from 0.17 to 0.25 with power remaining essentially unchanged. Therefore, fold asymmetry alone does not explain the observed behavior.

Table~\ref{tab:scalar-calibrators} presents class, seed, and corruption comparisons for Vector Platt, Shift-Aware Platt, temperature scaling, isotonic regression, histogram binning, and scalar Platt, all under the same label-free protocol. Unlike the pooled ECE in Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal}, these entries first compute ECE within each class--seed--corruption cell and then report the mean and standard deviation across cells; the two summaries therefore need not be numerically equal. LOIO has lower mean per-cell ECE in most comparisons (for example, MVTec $k=4$: LOIO $0.120\pm0.054$ versus isotonic $0.222\pm0.088$, temperature $0.240\pm0.104$, and histogram binning $0.229\pm0.099$), though the margin decreases at $k=8$. LOIO has lower ECE than isotonic regression in 55\% of MVTec cells and 62\% of VisA cells. Against Shift-Aware Platt on VisA $k=8$, the mean difference is $-0.011$ and LOIO has lower ECE in 51\% of cells. We do not report historical unadjusted significance values because a complete multiplicity-adjusted analysis is unavailable, and we make no confirmatory significance claims. Equal-mass ECE increases LOIO's per-cell error compared with equal-width ECE, highlighting the importance of the operational analyses above.

ECE on $1-p$ is sensitive to anomaly prevalence, even when ranking remains fixed. On the full VisA dataset, LOIO ECE decreases from 0.404 at 1\% anomaly prevalence to 0.149 at 50\%, and the ordering between LOIO and weighted methods reverses across this range. As a result, anomaly-balanced ECE cannot guarantee deployment reliability. Throughout this paper, FAR, power, precision, and risk--coverage are used for operational interpretation.

\subsection{Historical, Non-Independent CRESS Sensitivity}\label{sec:cress-historical}
These analyses were conducted before the strict nested protocol and are included solely as empirical sensitivity checks. Because they reuse source evidence and use pointwise rather than simultaneous intervals, they do not supersede the category-level result in Section~\ref{sec:cress-strict-results}.

Across all 15 label-stratified MVTec classes at $k=4$ (seeds 0--2, five conditions, 675 evaluation cells), matched-condition CRESS achieves mean power of 0.216 and 0.416 at $\alpha=0.05$ and 0.10, respectively, with the target-only anchor inactive. Mean FAR is 0.050/0.105/0.216 at $\alpha=0.05/0.10/0.20$, precision exceeds 0.90, and no-harm rates are 89\%/82\%/89\%. While the historical empirical gate passes overall, some condition-level exceptions persist: JPEG FAR is 0.121 at $\alpha=0.10$, and Gaussian noise and JPEG reach 0.226/0.235 at $\alpha=0.20$, exceeding the $\alpha+0.02$ thresholds.

Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-source-validated} also shows the power--FAR trade-off once the target-only anchor becomes active. At $\alpha=0.20$, historical CRESS lowers FAR relative to the anchor in every condition by 0.058--0.234, but its power is also lower by 0.094--0.365. The largest simultaneous FAR reduction and power loss occur under Gaussian noise. These paired changes support an empirical threshold-selection trade-off, not dominance of CRESS over the anchor.

Supporting tables are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}. At $k=8$, matched-condition CRESS achieves pooled power of 0.547/0.693 at the two nominal levels below the target-only floor. Its pooled FAR of 0.052/0.121/0.230 and no-harm rates of 80\%/74\%/81\% meet the historical empirical budget only at $\alpha=0.05$ (Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-k8}). The condition breakdown is less uniform than the pooled values: at $\alpha=0.10$, clean, blur, and brightness/contrast remain within the $\alpha+0.02$ budget, whereas Gaussian noise and JPEG reach FAR 0.138 and 0.141. At $\alpha=0.20$, only clean remains within budget; blur is marginally above it at 0.221, and brightness/contrast, Gaussian noise, and JPEG reach 0.223/0.232/0.260. Sub-floor precision nevertheless remains high (0.936--0.976), so the failure is specifically false-alarm control rather than an absence of detected anomalies.

Smoothed randomized p-values also enable alarms below the deterministic floor but retain the target-only mismatch. On pooled MVTec at $\alpha=0.05/0.10$, randomization yields FAR 0.085/0.170 and power 0.216/0.431, whereas historical CRESS yields FAR 0.049/0.105 and power 0.216/0.416 (Table~\ref{tab:randomized-pvalue}). On VisA, randomization is more conservative (FAR 0.036/0.072 versus 0.051/0.095); its power is slightly higher at $\alpha=0.05$ (0.150 versus 0.144) but lower at $\alpha=0.10$ (0.300 versus 0.351). Thus neither route uniformly dominates across datasets and operating levels. The historical CRESS comparison remains non-independent and cannot be promoted to certified evidence.

Within-VisA sources yield FAR 0.051/0.095/0.192 and power 0.144/0.351/0.637, with precision 0.847/0.843/0.810 and no-harm 89\%/84\%/78\% (Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-visa}). Replacing them with MVTec sources lowers FAR to 0.013/0.040/0.098 but also lowers power to 0.091/0.201/0.415; precision rises to 0.936/0.881/0.852 and no-harm to 97\%/93\%/93\%. The foreign archive is therefore more conservative in this single transfer direction, but the accompanying power loss prevents interpreting it as a general safety property.

\subsection{Secondary Deployment and Detector Diagnostics}
The risk--coverage analysis in Figure~\ref{fig:risk-coverage} ranks images by predictive entropy. Abstaining from the most uncertain 20\% reduces LOIO ECE from 0.068 to 0.030 on full MVTec but only from 0.077 to 0.075 on VisA; VisA reaches 0.066 at 70\% coverage. The benefit is therefore dataset-dependent rather than a uniform consequence of abstention. This remains a selective-operation diagnostic, not a formal certificate.

Table~\ref{tab:protocol-routing} evaluates protocol-locked representative routing. Fixed LOIO reduces ECE from 0.301--0.361 to 0.099--0.154 across the two transfer directions, LOCO, and within-split evaluation; the corresponding $\Delta$ECE ranges from $-0.207$ to $-0.188$, with 100\% no-harm in the reported cells. This supports using a simple fixed target-only route as the main baseline without validation-label expert selection. Because the table covers representative locked protocols and ECE only, it does not establish that LOIO dominates learned or weighted routing under every dataset, shift, or operational metric.

Table~\ref{tab:pixel-diagnostics} separates localization from robustness. PCA64 exceeds the controlled memory bank in pixel AUROC by only 0.004/0.006/0.008 at $k=1/4/8$, respectively. AU-PRO exposes a complementary trade-off: PCA64 is lower at $k=1$ (0.820 versus 0.845), tied at $k=4$ (0.862), and higher at $k=8$ (0.876 versus 0.863). The low-storage ranker is therefore competitive in this controlled localization comparison, but the small and metric-dependent differences do not support a broad localization-superiority claim.

The fragility block is less favorable. A label-aware PCA-residual FGSM surrogate reduces image AUROC from 0.937/0.945 to 0.158/0.175 at $\varepsilon=2/255$, below chance because the score ordering is inverted. The degradation is non-monotonic: at $8/255$, AUROC recovers to 0.446 for both $k$ values. This behavior indicates sensitivity to the surrogate objective and attack setting itself; it is a diagnostic and does not support an adversarial-robustness claim.
