\begin{abstract}
Few-shot industrial anomaly detectors built on frozen foundation features can rank defects accurately, but their thresholds need not retain the same false-alarm meaning across categories or input shifts. We audit this reliability problem separately from ranking. Conformal Reliability Routing (CRR) retains a low-storage DINOv2 PCA residual and adds label-free leave-one-image-out (LOIO) p-values from $k$ normal supports. On 15 MVTec and 12 VisA categories under four corruptions, the audit exposes an exact resolution floor: target-only alarms cannot fire below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$, while empirical normal p-values change from conservative on VisA to anti-conservative on corrupted MVTec. We then evaluate Source-Conditioned Cross-Category Reliability Routing (SC3R), which uses disjoint reference, proposal, and certification source categories. A frozen grid covering $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, five seeds, five conditions, within-dataset tests, and MVTec-to-VisA/MPDD transfer yields a decisive negative result: category-level Hoeffding certification selects the zero no-alarm threshold in all 960 operational gate cells. We derive the corresponding feasibility condition; even with zero empirical loss and one candidate, certification at $\alpha=0.20/0.10/0.05$ requires at least 60/240/958 independent categories, far beyond the three or four certification categories available here. Fixed-archive image certificates select positive thresholds in 37--60\% of settings, but target a different estimand and cannot replace the failed new-category certificate. Historical non-independent SC3R results remain useful empirical diagnostics, not certified evidence. The contribution is therefore an auditable reliability framework and a quantitative boundary on what few-category source archives can certify, rather than an unconditional target-control claim.
\end{abstract}
