\section{Conclusion and future work}\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced Conformal Reliability Routing, a decoupled framework for low-storage few-shot industrial anomaly detection. Its central contribution is a pair of problem and mechanism. The problem is structural: a conformal alarm calibrated on $k$ normal supports cannot fire below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$, and exact discrete uniformity tests show that at its first attainable operating point the target-only rule is conservative on VisA but anti-conservative on corrupted MVTec. The mechanism is SC3R, a source-validated cross-category thresholding scheme: it passes a pre-registered gate on all 15 MVTec classes at $k{=}4$---tracking nominal false-alarm rates almost exactly below and at the floor without target anomaly labels, with CI-supported power gains under every corruption condition---replicates on all 12 VisA classes, and transfers conservatively when the source archive comes from a different dataset entirely, trading power for false-alarm rates that stay under budget. Supporting these claims, CRR preserves frozen DINOv2 PCA/subspace residuals for anomaly ranking while a separate label-free reliability layer supplies decision signals, and the LOIO conformal route reduces calibration error on both full corruption benchmarks (overall ECE 0.0766 on VisA, 0.0684 on MVTec) against the complete standard toolbox---Vector and Shift-Aware Platt as well as temperature scaling, isotonic regression, and histogram binning fit on the identical calibration set---with paired Wilcoxon support in every comparison except Shift-Aware Platt at VisA $k{=}8$. We also report the boundaries honestly: at $k{=}8$ heavy-tailed corruptions erode the false-alarm budget above $\alpha{=}0.05$, and cross-dataset archives halve the sub-floor power. The resulting story is deliberately focused: CRR is not an MVTec AUROC SOTA or adversarial-robustness method; it is a calibrated, storage-light reliability layer for few-shot inspection systems where raw anomaly scores alone are not sufficient for deployment-quality decisions. More broadly, the results characterize a property of few-shot learned detectors themselves: what their conformal alarms can promise is bounded by an exact resolution floor, their failure direction under shift is testable, and cross-category structure the learning system already possesses is enough to restore validity below that floor.

Future work is scoped by the boundaries above: SC3R with more seeds and per-condition source validation to close the Gaussian/JPEG budget gap, and an audited vision--language baseline, which currently blocks on the absence of an official WinCLIP implementation.
