\section{Conclusion and future work}\label{sec:conclusion}
We have presented an auditable separation between low-storage anomaly ranking and few-shot alarm reliability. Three conclusions have emerged. First, a target-only conformal alarm built from $k$ calibration scores cannot fire below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$; zero alarms below this floor are a resolution artifact, not evidence of false-alarm control. Second, crossing the floor does not guarantee stability: corrupted normal inputs can make empirical false-alarm behavior anti-conservative without any change to the underlying ranker. Third, source-assisted category certification is limited by the number of independent categories, not by the number of images or support seeds. In the strict CRESS evaluation, every category-certified target cell receives $\tau=0$, and all 960 gate configurations fail across MVTec, VisA, and two transfer directions. This is not merely Hoeffding looseness: even without multiplicity, a deterministic distribution-free 95\% category-risk upper bound requires at least 14 all-zero categories to reach $\alpha=0.20$, compared with the three or four available. Fixed-archive image certificates and historical CRESS results provide sensitivity evidence for different or non-independent estimands; neither establishes certified target-category control.

The practical contribution is therefore an audit discipline rather than an unconditional deployment guarantee: report attainable rather than nominal operating points, test normal false alarms under declared shifts, specify the certification unit before observing results, and count independent categories rather than repeated views. CRESS remains a source-assisted thresholding and certification procedure, but its category-level certificate is infeasible at the archive sizes studied here. Future work must either collect category-rich source archives or introduce pre-specified parametric, hierarchical, or side-information assumptions with independent validity checks; a nominally tighter distribution-free bound cannot overcome the all-zero category limit with only three or four categories. Prospective evaluation should additionally cover real production shifts, temporal dependence, condition-estimation error, and end-to-end deployment cost.
