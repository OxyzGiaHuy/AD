\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Problem Setup}
For each category $c$, we observe a support set $\mathcal{S}_c={x_i}{i=1}^{k}$ containing only normal images. The test set includes both normal and anomalous images, but anomaly labels are reserved exclusively for evaluation and are not utilized in fitting the main detector or calibrator. The objectives are to generate: (i) a raw anomaly ranking score $s(x)$, (ii) a probability-like reliability score $\hat{p}(y=1\mid x)$ or conformal anomaly confidence, and (iii) optional pixel-level maps. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} provides an overview: a frozen backbone supplies a ranking lane that remains unaltered, a label-free target-only reliability lane whose conformal alarms reach the attainable-alpha floor, and a CRESS lane that attempts source-assisted operation below that floor but yields a zero threshold when certification is inadequate.

\subsection{Frozen Patch Features and Subspace Ranking}
We extract patch features with a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone. Let $z_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote patch $j$ from image $i$. A PCA/subspace model is fit on support patches. With support mean $\mu$ and retained principal directions $U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$, the patch residual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
r(z)=\left|(z-\mu)-UU^{\top}(z-\mu)\right|2 .
\end{equation}
Patch residuals form an anomaly map. The image-level ranking score is a high-residual aggregation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
    s(x)=\operatorname{Agg}{j}, r(z_j),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Agg}$ denotes the patch maximum in the clean benchmark of ranking accuracy and storage and the mean of the top-$1\%$ patch residuals ($\rho{=}0.01$) in the conformal pipeline; the top-fraction variant serves as a smoothed maximum to stabilize LOIO calibration scores. We state this explicitly because the two protocols employ different aggregation methods, and Section~\ref{sec:results} reports an aggregation-sensitivity ablation. Notably, this raw score is the quantity used for AUROC and AP, and calibration procedures do not alter the ranking score.

\subsection{Vector and Shift-Aware Calibration}
The first reliability family is post-hoc logistic calibration. A scalar Platt scaler maps $s(x)$ to a probability, while Vector Platt uses a richer descriptor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
    \phi(x)=\left[s_{\mathrm{pca}}(x),; s_{\mathrm{head}}(x),; |\tilde{s}{\mathrm{pca}}(x)-\tilde{s}{\mathrm{head}}(x)|\right],
\end{equation}
where $s_{\mathrm{head}}$ is a lightweight synthetic-anomaly head score and tildes denote normalized scores. The head is a small MLP ($384{\to}256{\to}1$, dropout 0.1) trained with binary cross-entropy to separate support normal patches from synthetically perturbed patches; a patch perturbation resamples a support patch and adds Gaussian noise plus a sign shift in feature space. Its image-level score is the maximum patch probability. The head participates only in the calibration descriptor $\phi(x)$, never in the anomaly ranking. The calibrated probability is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:platt}
    \hat{p}_{\theta}(x)=\sigma(w^{\top}\phi(x)+b).
\end{equation}
Shift-Aware Platt augments $\phi(x)$ by incorporating five shift descriptors: the maximum and mean distance of test patches to the support feature center (scaled by the support 95th-percentile norm), the mean and standard deviation of patch residual scores, and a residual concentration ratio (maximum over mean). Weighted Platt reweights calibration samples by density-ratio estimates from a logistic domain classifier, following weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift~\cite{tibshirani2019covariate}. While these variants are effective for structured shifts, they may over-adapt, thereby motivating the adoption of a conservative conformal route.

\emph{Calibrator training protocol and label source.} All calibrators in the primary protocol are trained without real anomaly labels (mode \texttt{normal\_synthetic}). The calibration set consists of the $k$ support normal images (label 0) and an equal number of synthetic images (label 1) obtained by replacing a random $25\%$ of a support image's patch features with perturbed support patches as described above. Vector-family calibrators fit a multivariate logistic model on $\phi(x)$ using gradient descent with $L_2$ regularization. The memory-bank and plain-subspace baselines, which do not utilize a calibration descriptor, apply scalar Platt scaling to the raw score under the same synthetic protocol. Standard scalar calibrators evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:results} - temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}, isotonic regression~\cite{zadrozny2002transforming}, and histogram binning~\cite{zadrozny2001obtaining} - are fit per class on the identical calibration set, mapping the raw score $s(x)$ to a probability. Since raw residuals are not logits, temperature scaling is applied after centering at the midpoint of the two calibration class means, $\hat{p}(x)=\sigma((s(x)-c)/T)$, with only $T$ learned. An \texttt{anomaly\_val} mode that calibrates on $10\%$ held-out real anomalies is reported solely as an explicitly labeled upper bound. This symmetric, label-free protocol ensures a fair LOIO-versus-calibrator comparison: every family sees exactly the same $k$ support normals and no test information.

\subsection{LOIO Conformal Reliability}
For $k\ge2$, the target-only route constructs normal calibration residuals by leave-one-image-out support splitting. For each support image $x_i$, a subspace is fit on $\mathcal{S}_c\setminus\{x_i\}$ and the held-out image receives a residual score $s_{-i}(x_i)$. The resulting set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rloio}
    \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOIO}}=\{s_{-i}(x_i):x_i\in\mathcal{S}_c\}
\end{equation}
serves as normal nonconformity evidence. For a test image $x$, the conformal p-value is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loio}
    p_{\mathrm{LOIO}}(x)=\frac{1+\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOIO}}}\mathbf{1}\{r\ge s(x)\}}{1+|\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOIO}}|}.
\end{equation}
We report $p_{\mathrm{conf}}(x)=1-p_{\mathrm{LOIO}}(x)$ as a probability-like anomaly reliability view. It should be interpreted as relative extremeness against held-out support normals, not as a fully supervised posterior probability. Weighted conformal replaces the uniform count by density-ratio weights~\cite{tibshirani2019covariate} and reports effective sample size; empirically, it is less stable than LOIO on the main benchmark.

\emph{Exchangeability caveat.} The construction above is deliberately asymmetric: each calibration residual $s_{-i}(x_i)$ comes from a subspace fit on $k{-}1$ images, while the test score $s(x)$ uses all $k$. The two statistics are therefore not exactly exchangeable, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:loio} is a leave-one-out approximation in the spirit of cross-conformal and jackknife+ inference~\cite{vovk2005,barber2021jackknife,hennhofer2024loio} rather than a split-conformal p-value with a formal finite-sample guarantee~\cite{bates2023testing,laxhammar2015inductive}. We handle this empirically on two fronts. First, a matched-LOIO audit scores held-out support and test images under the same fold-specific subspaces (pairing $s_{-i}(x)$ with $r_{-i}$, the jackknife+ pairing): it does not improve the operational false-alarm/power trade-off (Section~\ref{sec:results}), so the simpler construction is retained. Second, rather than assuming validity, we audit it directly: Section~\ref{sec:results} reports discrete-grid diagnostics of the normal p-values. Their reference distribution is simulated under an idealized exchangeable null while preserving shared-calibration dependence within clusters defined by class and seed; it localizes empirical deviations but is not proof of exchangeability.

\subsection{Attainable Alpha and the Few-Shot Resolution Floor}
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (attainable-alpha floor).} For any finite calibration scores $r_1,\ldots,r_k$, a p-value of the form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:loio} belongs to $\{j/(k{+}1):j=1,\ldots,k{+}1\}$. Hence the alarm $\mathbf{1}\{p(x)\leq\alpha\}$ is identically zero for $\alpha<1/(k{+}1)$.

\noindent\emph{Proof.} The exceedance count $\sum_i\mathbf{1}\{r_i\geq s(x)\}$ is an integer between 0 and $k$. Adding one and dividing by $k{+}1$ gives the stated grid and lower bound. No exchangeability assumption is needed for this algebraic claim. \hfill$\square$

For $k{=}4$, this floor is $0.2$; for $k{=}8$, it is $1/9\approx0.111$. The resolution limit is made explicit through an attainable-alpha analysis, which, for each nominal $\alpha$, reports the nearest attainable grid point and the corresponding empirical false-alarm rate. This structural property delineates the regime where cross-category support may be beneficial; it is not claimed as an algorithmic innovation. The standard approach to address discreteness is a smoothed (randomized) conformal p-value, which employs an independent uniform tie-break and achieves uniformity under the relevant exchangeability assumptions. Section~\ref{sec:results} evaluates this method as a baseline, noting that randomization overcomes the floor but does not resolve the observed shift between support and test data.

\subsection{Cross-Category Reliability Estimation with Source Support (CRESS)}
In multi-category industrial settings, normal images from \emph{other} categories, referred to as source classes, may be abundant even when the target category contains only $k$ normal supports. CRESS leverages these source images without requiring target anomaly labels. To ensure comparability, target and source residuals are normalized: each raw score is centered and scaled using the robust median and MAD statistics of its respective class’s LOIO support residuals. This normalization serves as an empirical comparability mechanism rather than evidence that class distributions coincide. In matched-condition mode, source images share the target’s corruption condition, necessitating condition metadata. Clean-source mode exclusively utilizes clean normals. The condition-agnostic ablation computes the median score across all available condition views for each base source image, ensuring that repeated corruptions are not treated as independent observations; deterministic mismatched-condition routing functions solely as a negative control. Pooling source normals enables finer p-value resolution than the $k$ target supports and allows for thresholds below $1/(k{+}1)$.

\subsection{Nested Source Certification and Transfer Boundary}
For a fixed target, source classes are partitioned before certification into reference classes $\mathcal{R}$, proposal classes $\mathcal{P}$, and certification classes $\mathcal{C}$. Scores from $\mathcal{R}$ define conformal p-values. The distinct p-values on $\mathcal{P}$ propose at most $M$ positive candidate thresholds. For candidate $\tau$, certification unit $i$ has a loss $L_i(\tau)\in[0,1]$. An image unit uses its Bernoulli alarm indicator and targets risk for the fixed source-image mixture. A class unit averages alarms within a held-out source class and targets a new draw from a source-category meta-population. Let $A$ be the number of reported $\alpha$ levels. For the class unit with $n$ classes, CRESS uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sc3r-ucb}
 U_{\mathrm{H}}(\tau)=\min\!\left\{1,\;\bar L(\tau)+\sqrt{\frac{\log(2AM/\delta)}{2n}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For independent Bernoulli image alarms, we use the exact one-sided binomial (Clopper-Pearson) upper bound with tail probability $\delta/(2AM)$. The factor $2A$ allocates the family error across both units and all reported levels; $M$ allocates it across candidates. CRESS selects the largest proposed $\tau$ whose corresponding upper bound is at most $\alpha$. If none passes, $\tau=0$ gives a deterministic no-alarm fallback. Reference, proposal, and certification classes are disjoint; target test images and labels enter none of these stages.

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2 (conditional source certificate).} Conditional on the reference and proposal stages, suppose certification units are independent draws from the declared source-unit population: Bernoulli losses for the image analysis, or bounded losses in $[0,1]$ for the class analysis. Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously across the $A$ levels and both unit definitions, the corresponding source risk of every selected threshold is at most its $\alpha$.

\noindent\emph{Proof.} For a fixed level, unit, and candidate, the one-sided Clopper-Pearson construction or Hoeffding's inequality fails with probability at most $\delta/(2AM)$. A union bound over $M$ candidates, $A$ levels, and two units gives total failure probability at most $\delta$; no independence between these bounds is required. Selection occurs only on this simultaneous event, so each passing candidate has risk at most its level. The zero fallback has zero alarm risk. \hfill$\square$

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 3 (Hoeffding feasibility).} Under the category-level Hoeffding rule above, a positive candidate can pass at level $\alpha$ only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sc3r-feasibility}
 n \ge \frac{\log(2AM/\delta)}{2\alpha^2}.
\end{equation}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} Because $\bar L(\tau)\ge0$, the unclipped upper bound is at least $\sqrt{\log(2AM/\delta)/(2n)}$. Requiring it to be no larger than $\alpha$ and rearranging gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:sc3r-feasibility}. Any positive empirical loss only increases the requirement. \hfill$\square$


\noindent\textbf{Proposition 4 (distribution-free all-zero lower bound).}
Let $\beta\in(0,1)$ and let $L_1,\ldots,L_n$ be iid losses from an arbitrary distribution $P$ on $[0,1]$, with mean $\mu_P$. Let $U_n:[0,1]^n\to[0,1]$ be any deterministic upper confidence bound satisfying
\[
    \inf_{P}\Pr_{P^n}\!\left\{\mu_P\le U_n(L_1,\ldots,L_n)\right\}\ge 1-\beta .
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distribution-free-feasibility}
    U_n(0,\ldots,0)\ge 1-\beta^{1/n}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, even after observing zero loss in every certification category, $U_n\le\alpha$ is possible only if
\[
    n\ge \frac{\log\beta}{\log(1-\alpha)}.
\]

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Write $u=U_n(0,\ldots,0)$ and suppose, for contradiction, that $u<1-\beta^{1/n}$. Choose a Bernoulli distribution with mean $q$ such that $u<q<1-\beta^{1/n}$; Bernoulli laws are a subclass of distributions on $[0,1]$. Its all-zero sample has probability $(1-q)^n>\beta$. On that event $U_n=u<q=\mu_P$, so the bound fails with probability greater than $\beta$, contradicting uniform $(1-\beta)$ coverage. Rearranging $1-\beta^{1/n}\le\alpha$ gives the sample-size condition. \hfill$\square$

Proposition~4 shows that the frozen failure is not solely an artifact of Hoeffding's looseness. Even for one fixed candidate, one level, no image-unit co-reporting, and no multiplicity penalty ($\beta=\delta=0.05$), any deterministic uniformly valid distribution-free bound based only on iid category losses needs at least 14, 29, and 59 categories at $\alpha=0.20$, $0.10$, and $0.05$. Under the frozen equal allocation $\beta=\delta/(2AM)$ with $A=3$ and the most favorable $M=1$, the corresponding lower limits are 22, 46, and 94; the declared Hoeffding rule requires 60, 240, and 958. Every requirement exceeds the available three or four certification categories.

Proposition~4 is intentionally limited in scope: it addresses deterministic, uniformly valid, distribution-free upper bounds that rely solely on iid bounded category losses. Parametric or hierarchical assumptions, informative side data, or randomized confidence procedures are excluded from its statement and would necessitate separate validity arguments. This elementary minimax argument is not introduced as new confidence-bound theory; its purpose is to preclude the misinterpretation that the observed phenomenon is solely due to the looseness of Hoeffding’s inequality. The calculation further demonstrates that adding source images or repeated support seeds cannot compensate for a lack of independent categories: such additions may refine the fixed-archive image analysis but do not increase the category-level $n$. Additionally, altering the certification unit after observing the result would modify the estimand and does not constitute a valid adjustment to the frozen protocol.

\noindent\textbf{Corollary 1 (conditional target transfer).} In the event described in Proposition~2, the target risk is also at most $\alpha$ if, for every proposed threshold, the target normal alarm probability is no larger than the corresponding source-unit risk. The same conclusion follows if the target category is exchangeable with the certification-category meta-population.

The corollary’s dominance or category-exchangeability condition is an assumption, not a consequence of robust normalization or matched routing. The two source certificates answer different questions: an image certificate for a fixed archive mixture cannot be relabeled a new-category guarantee, while a class certificate with few categories may be vacuous and cannot be replaced by the image result. The frozen nested results in Section~\ref{sec:results} exhibit exactly this category-level failure; the older non-independent results remain labeled historical empirical diagnostics. A direct pooled-source conformal baseline uses the entire routed source pool with threshold $\alpha$ and receives no artificial data restriction; unlike CRESS, it performs no proposal or certification step. Section~\ref{sec:cress-strict-results} reports it as an uncertified empirical reference.

The broader routing component draws inspiration from the shared-versus-specialized principle in gated-expert systems. Candidate reliability views include Vector Platt, Shift-Aware Platt, weighted Platt, LOIO conformal, and weighted conformal. We evaluate fixed routes, validation-ECE selection, and simple no-label rules based on shift descriptors and effective sample size. Because learned gates did not consistently outperform fixed LOIO across all settings, the main paper designates fixed LOIO as the target-only reference and treats learned gates as diagnostic ablations; these are not characterized as safe routes under distribution shift.

\subsection{Selective Diagnostics}
In addition to generating a calibrated alarm score, the reliability framework records entropy, expert disagreement, conformal confidence, and effective sample size as selective-risk indicators. These metrics do not influence the anomaly ranking but instead support deployment diagnostics, including abstention, risk-versus-coverage analysis, and manual review of low-confidence alarms.
