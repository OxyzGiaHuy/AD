\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Datasets and Few-Shot Splits}
We conduct evaluations on MVTec AD~\cite{mvtec2019} and VisA~\cite{visa2022}; the strict external-transfer audit additionally incorporates MPDD. MVTec AD comprises industrial object and texture categories with pixel-level defect masks. VisA contains a broader range of industrial categories and serves as the primary benchmark for full-scale corruption and calibration-shift. MPDD provides six metal-part categories for an external MVTec-to-MPDD stress test; within-MPDD category certification is not pursued due to the insufficient number of categories for a three-way source split. For each class, support sets are sampled from normal training images using fixed seeds. Unless otherwise specified, $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ is employed for the clean study of accuracy and storage and for the strict nested studies, whereas the earlier conformal corruption tables utilize $k\in\{4,8\}$.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocols}
We structure the experiments into six protocol groups. The first five address clean accuracy and storage, PCA64 and PCA128 sensitivity, corruption reliability, representative routing, and attainable operating levels. The sixth protocol is the frozen strict nested protocol, which uses $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, seeds from 0 to 4, $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, and includes clean data as well as Gaussian noise, blur, brightness/contrast, and JPEG corruptions. Each cell contains at most 120 evaluation images, with $\rho=0.01$, PCA64, $\delta=0.05$, and up to 20 candidate thresholds. This protocol evaluates MVTec and VisA within-dataset, MVTec-to-VisA, and MVTec-to-MPDD, using matched, clean-source, condition-agnostic, and mismatched negative-control routing. The operational gate requires a nonzero category-certified threshold in at least 80\% of target cells, a target false alarm rate (FAR) of at most $\alpha+0.02$, nonzero power, at least 80\% no-harm, and a positive lower bound on simultaneous power gain. Zero thresholds and failed cells are retained.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablations}
We compare against a controlled nearest-neighbor memory bank utilizing DINOv2, a SubspaceAD-style PCA residual baseline, scalar Platt, Vector Platt, Shift-Aware Platt, weighted Platt, temperature scaling, isotonic regression, histogram binning, LOIO conformal, and weighted conformal. For the source-assisted study, direct pooled-source conformal serves as an uncertified baseline: it uses the complete routed source pool with $\tau=\alpha$ but performs no reference, proposal, or certification split and applies no UCB gate. The controlled memory-bank implementation does not constitute an official reproduction of PatchCore or AnomalyDINO; it maintains the frozen DINOv2 feature pipeline and fixed data split to isolate scoring and storage trade-offs. Additionally, the official AnomalyDINO code (ViT-S/14 at 448px, agnostic preprocessing) is executed on both MVTec and VisA (official 1-class split) for $k\in\{1,4,8\}$ with three seeds, and results are reported as a separate accuracy reference. WinCLIP~\cite{winclip2023} is included as the vision-language representative, but only as reported numbers, since no official implementation is available. Auditing the leading community reimplementation under its protocol (ViT-B-16+240, LAION-400M; zero-shot and 1-shot with fixed support lists, three runs) yielded 71.7/77.4 mean image AUROC on MVTec and 66.0/70.0 on VisA, compared to the reported 91.8/93.1 and 78.1/83.8, indicating a roughly 20-point gap that the reimplementation's authors also acknowledge and cannot attribute. Consequently, claim rules distinguish audited rows from reported rows, and WinCLIP is entered in Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} as a cited reference rather than an audited baseline. Official SubspaceAD representative runs are included as a novelty guardrail. Their strong performance demonstrates that frozen DINOv2 subspace residuals constitute an existing competitive ranking mechanism; the residual is inherited, and the contribution of this paper commences with the reliability of the alarms constructed upon it.

\subsection{Metrics}
Ranking metrics are distinguished from reliability metrics. AUROC and AP are calculated from the raw PCA or subspace residual score $s(x)$. Pixel-level localization is assessed using pixel AUROC and AU-PRO when masks are available. Reliability is evaluated operationally by empirical false-alarm rate at fixed $\alpha$, detection power, alarm precision, discrete uniformity of normal p-values, and risk-versus-coverage curves. Expected calibration error (ECE), Brier score, and negative log-likelihood (NLL) are reported as secondary metrics based on the selected reliability probability or probability-like conformal view. Storage is quantified as retained reference or model state per class. Robustness experiments include corruption shift and a label-aware PCA-residual FGSM surrogate, with the latter serving exclusively as a fragility diagnostic.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Claim Rules}
All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU using PyTorch 2.12.1, with DINOv2 patch features cached in fp32 format. For reproducibility, the frozen commit, DINOv2 source and weight hashes, environment specifications, dataset counts, per-image predictions, base-image identities, support manifests, corruption parameters, source partitions, candidate-threshold bounds, and SHA-256 checksums are documented. Automated integrity checks encompass 1,500 MVTec, 1,200 VisA, and 600 MPDD view cells, identifying duplicate cells, missing support statistics, or overlap between support and test sets. Reliability methods cannot claim ranking improvement unless the raw score is altered; image-level and category-level certificates are not interchangeable.
