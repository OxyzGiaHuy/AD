\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Clean Accuracy and Storage}
\input{tables/tab_clean_efficiency}
Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} summarizes clean image-level performance and storage. The official AnomalyDINO reproduction (released code, 3 seeds) leads image AUROC at every $k$ on both datasets (MVTec 0.965/0.976/0.980; VisA 0.857/0.912/0.926, matching its published VisA numbers), which we report as an explicit accuracy reference: neither the controlled nearest-neighbor baseline nor CRR claims ranking superiority. CalibSubspaceHead is competitive with the controlled DINOv2 nearest-neighbor memory-bank baseline while being far more compact: PCA64 stores about 0.472 MB, compared with roughly 2--6 MB for the controlled memory bank. On VisA, the detector is stronger relative to this controlled baseline, and the PCA128 ablation improves VisA AUROC by 0.011--0.016 over PCA64 while keeping storage below 0.6 MB; a paired class--seed hierarchical bootstrap confirms this gain is statistically supported, with 95\% intervals excluding zero at every $k$ ($k{=}1$: $[+0.003,+0.020]$; $k{=}2$: $[+0.006,+0.026]$; $k{=}4$: $[+0.009,+0.024]$; $k{=}8$: $[+0.010,+0.019]$). The controlled rows must not be read as official PatchCore or AnomalyDINO reproductions; only the official AnomalyDINO rows are. Two entries in Table~\ref{tab:clean-efficiency} deserve explicit anatomy so they are not mistaken for placeholders or a strawman. Under the label-free scalar-Platt protocol, the controlled memory-bank baseline's probability collapses to a constant: at $k\le2$ every support patch self-matches (distance zero, probability $\approx$1), while at $k\ge4$ the 4096-patch memory cap forces subsampling (probability $\approx$0). Its ECE therefore equals a prevalence complement (0.277/0.693) with Brier $\approx$ ECE---the signature of a constant probability---and its storage saturates at exactly 6.000\,MB (4096 patches $\times$ 384 dims $\times$ 4 bytes). These values diagnose that raw nearest-neighbor distances resist naive label-free calibration; they are not competitive calibration results.

\emph{Storage accounting and compute cost.} The per-class figures above cover only the class-specific reference state and deliberately exclude the shared frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone ($\approx$22M parameters, $\approx$84\,MB in fp32), which every compared method requires once per deployment. The 0.472\,MB CRR state decomposes into the PCA subspace (64 components $\times$ 384 dims in fp32 $\approx$ 0.098\,MB, matching the observed 0.094\,MB PCA128--PCA64 increment) and the synthetic-anomaly head and calibrator ($\approx$0.37\,MB); the controlled memory bank stores up to 4096 patch vectors ($\le$6.0\,MB). At inference, cached-feature scoring costs about 1.3\,ms per image for the subspace detector versus 5--13\,ms for the nearest-neighbor bank, while the shared backbone forward dominates end-to-end latency (order of seconds per batch on first pass, identical across methods). Fitting the $k$ leave-one-out subspaces for LOIO adds a one-time per-class setup cost of $k$ PCA fits on at most $k{\times}1369$ patch vectors, i.e., well under a second at $k\le8$.

\input{tables/tab_agg_ablation}
\emph{Aggregation sensitivity.} Because the clean benchmark aggregates patch residuals by max while the conformal pipeline uses the top-1\% mean, Table~\ref{tab:agg-ablation} quantifies the sensitivity of the raw ranking to this choice on representative classes. The spread across aggregators is 0.01--0.02 AUROC: moderate top-$\rho$ pooling ($\rho\in[0.005,0.02]$) is slightly better than max on MVTec, while very wide pooling ($\rho{=}0.05$) hurts VisA. Both configurations used in the paper sit within 0.01--0.02 of the per-setting optimum, so no conclusion in this paper depends on the aggregator choice.

\subsection{Full VisA Conformal Reliability}
\input{tables/tab_visa_full_conformal}
Table~\ref{tab:visa-full-conformal} gives the main calibration-error result. Throughout the paper, ECE uses 15 equal-width bins; because few-shot conformal confidences occupy at most $k{+}1$ distinct values, we also report an equal-mass adaptive ECE below, and Section~\ref{sec:uniformity} replaces ECE with conformal-native diagnostics for the central claim. Across 56,000 full VisA corruption rows, LOIO conformal obtains pooled ECE 0.0766 while preserving the same PCA/subspace ranking. The strongest regime is $k=4$, where pooled ECE drops to 0.0391. At $k=8$, ECE increases to 0.1140 but remains better than Platt-style baselines in every corruption cell. The pooled reliability diagrams in Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} visualize both effects: confidences occupy only the $k{+}1$ attainable levels, and the gap below the diagonal---overconfidence on normal images---widens from $k{=}4$ to $k{=}8$ and is larger on MVTec than on VisA. The uniformity analysis in Section~\ref{sec:uniformity} explains this $k$ trend mechanistically: with a clean support and corrupted test images, normal test residuals are inflated relative to the clean LOIO calibration set; the tighter $k{=}8$ subspace amplifies this mismatch, pushing normal p-values anti-conservative.

\input{tables/tab_visa_ece_by_corruption}
Table~\ref{tab:visa-ece-corruption} isolates corruption effects. LOIO conformal improves over Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt for blur, brightness/contrast shift, Gaussian noise, and JPEG at both $k=4$ and $k=8$. Weighted conformal is a useful ablation but not a safe default: it is competitive for selected $k=8$ structured corruptions, yet it is much worse at $k=4$ and under Gaussian noise.

\subsection{Full MVTec Conformal Reliability}
The same conformal protocol was run on all 15 MVTec classes ($k\in\{4,8\}$, seeds 0--2, four corruptions, 37{,}464 image rows). The VisA conclusion replicates: LOIO conformal reliability attains an overall ECE of 0.0684 (0.0600 at $k{=}4$, 0.0769 at $k{=}8$) while preserving the raw ranking (image AUROC 0.912), and it improves ECE over both Vector Platt and Shift-Aware Platt in every one of the eight $k$--corruption cells, often by a factor of two to four (e.g., $k{=}4$ Gaussian noise: 0.033 versus 0.233 and 0.270; Figure~\ref{fig:ece-corruption}). Weighted conformal is again markedly worse (ECE 0.31--0.45). Conformal reliability is therefore not a VisA-specific effect; it holds across both benchmarks under identical protocols.

\subsection{Statistical Validity: Uniformity Tests, Paired Significance, and Standard Calibrators}
\label{sec:uniformity}
Conformal p-values promise $P(p\le\alpha)\le\alpha$ on normal data under exchangeability---a false-alarm guarantee, not a posterior probability. We therefore test this property directly instead of relying on ECE. Because a $k$-shot LOIO p-value is discrete on the grid $\{j/(k{+}1)\}$, a continuous Kolmogorov--Smirnov test against $U(0,1)$ is invalid by construction; we instead compare the empirical CDF of normal p-values to its exact expectation at every grid point and calibrate the resulting discrete KS statistic against the exact null by Monte Carlo. Figure~\ref{fig:uniformity} shows the resulting discrete Q-Q view. Uniformity is rejected in all 16 dataset--$k$--corruption cells (Monte Carlo $p\le0.005$), but the \emph{direction} of the violation is the informative result. On VisA at $k{=}4$ the deviation is uniformly conservative (empirical CDF below nominal; worst gap $-0.10$ at the 0.4 grid point), consistent with the below-nominal false-alarm rates above. On VisA at $k{=}8$ and on MVTec at both $k$, the deviation flips anti-conservative under corruption---most severely for Gaussian noise on MVTec $k{=}4$, where $\hat{F}(0.2)=0.46$ against a nominal $0.20$. This is exactly the clean-support/corrupted-test mechanism anticipated by the exchangeability caveat in the method section: corruption inflates normal test residuals relative to the clean LOIO calibration set, and a tighter $k{=}8$ subspace amplifies the mismatch. A matched-LOIO audit that scores support and test under the same fold-specific subspaces (the jackknife+ pairing~\cite{barber2021jackknife}) does not repair this trade-off: on representative MVTec $k{=}4$ it raises clean false alarms from 0.17 to 0.25 with essentially unchanged power. The violation is driven by the support--test condition mismatch, not by the leave-one-out asymmetry alone---which is precisely the failure mode SC3R's matched-condition source validation addresses.

\input{tables/tab_scalar_calibrators}
Paired significance replaces point estimates for the headline calibration comparison, and the comparison now covers the full standard toolbox: Table~\ref{tab:scalar-calibrators} reports class--seed--corruption cell ECE for LOIO against Vector Platt, Shift-Aware Platt, and the standard scalar calibrators---temperature scaling, isotonic regression, and histogram binning---all fit on the identical label-free calibration set. LOIO conformal is significantly better than every scalar calibrator on both benchmarks and both $k$ (paired Wilcoxon $p\le2.5\times10^{-4}$ in all 16 comparisons, and $p\le4.8\times10^{-8}$ in 14 of them; e.g., MVTec $k{=}4$: LOIO $0.120\pm0.054$ versus isotonic $0.222\pm0.088$, temperature $0.240\pm0.104$, histogram binning $0.229\pm0.099$). Among the scalar family, isotonic regression is strongest and narrows the gap at $k{=}8$ (MVTec: delta $-0.023$, LOIO better in only 55\% of cells, though still $p{=}2.5\times10^{-4}$; VisA: delta $-0.035$, 62\%), which is expected: with $2k$ calibration points the nonparametric fit becomes more competitive as $k$ grows. Against Vector Platt, LOIO wins with $p\le4.9\times10^{-9}$ everywhere; against Shift-Aware Platt the result holds everywhere except VisA $k{=}8$, where the difference is not significant ($p{=}0.20$, LOIO better in 51\% of cells)---an honest boundary we state rather than average away. Replacing equal-width with equal-mass (adaptive) ECE, which cannot be flattered by at most $k{+}1$ occupied confidence levels, increases LOIO's per-cell ECE (MVTec: $0.120\to0.160$ at $k{=}4$, $0.135\to0.182$ at $k{=}8$; VisA: $0.132\to0.227$, $0.158\to0.236$) while leaving the ordering against all calibrator baselines intact. Binning does inflate the apparent margin, which is one more reason the operational analyses below, not ECE, carry the central claim.

\subsection{Attainable Alpha and Operational False-Alarm Control}
\input{tables/tab_attainable_alpha}
Calibration error alone does not answer the operator's question: ``if I alarm at $p\le\alpha$, how often is a good part flagged?'' Table~\ref{tab:attainable-alpha} reports the attainable-alpha analysis. The pattern is structural, not empirical: at $k{=}4$ every nominal level below $0.2$ raises zero alarms on both datasets because no attainable p-value exists below the floor $1/(k{+}1)$, and at $k{=}8$ the same holds below $1/9$. Reporting these zeros as ``conservative coverage'' would be misleading; they are resolution artifacts of the few-shot calibration set.

\input{tables/tab_false_alarm_control}
Table~\ref{tab:false-alarm-control} evaluates the first attainable operating point, $\alpha{=}0.20$, on both full benchmarks. On VisA the LOIO alarm rule is conservative but functional (VisA $k{=}4$: false-alarm 0.14--0.16 with detection 0.58--0.61 and precision around 0.81); on full MVTec the same rule is anti-conservative under corruption at $k{=}4$, which the SC3R analysis below addresses directly. We note a practical pitfall uncovered during this analysis: p-values stored in single precision can exceed their exact rational value (e.g., $1/5\rightarrow0.20000000298$), so an exact comparison $p\le\alpha$ silently discards every alarm at the floor. All thresholding in this paper uses a small tolerance, and we recommend the same in deployments.

\subsection{SC3R: Operating Below the Target-Only Alpha Floor}
\input{tables/tab_sc3r_source_validated}
Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-source-validated} evaluates SC3R against the target-only LOIO anchor on all 15 label-stratified MVTec classes ($k{=}4$, seeds 0--2, five conditions, 675 evaluation cells), under a pre-registered decision gate fixed before the run: nonzero power at $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10\}$, mean false-alarm rate at most $\alpha{+}0.02$, no-harm versus the anchor in at least 80\% of class--seed--corruption cells, no target anomaly labels, and hierarchical class--seed bootstrap support. Matched-condition SC3R passes every criterion. It achieves mean power 0.216 at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and 0.416 at $\alpha{=}0.10$ in a regime where the target-only anchor is structurally silent, while mean false-alarm rates track nominal almost exactly: 0.050, 0.105, and 0.216 at $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, with per-condition false-alarm rates between 0.048 and 0.051 at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and alarm precision above 0.90 throughout. No-harm rates are 89\%, 82\%, and 89\%. The hierarchical class--seed bootstrap is decisive at this scale: the 95\% confidence interval for the power gain over the anchor excludes zero for \emph{every} condition at both sub-floor levels (e.g., clean $\alpha{=}0.10$: $[0.30,0.64]$; JPEG $\alpha{=}0.05$: $[0.02,0.28]$). At $\alpha{=}0.20$, at the anchor's floor, the roles reverse in an instructive way: the anchor attains higher power but its false-alarm rate inflates to 0.27--0.46 under corruption, well above nominal, whereas SC3R remains within 0.005--0.035 of nominal. Source validation thus buys consistent false-alarm tracking at every level, at the cost of some power at levels the anchor can reach. Residual weaknesses are small and localized: JPEG at $\alpha{=}0.10$ (0.121) and Gaussian noise/JPEG at $\alpha{=}0.20$ (0.226/0.235) exceed the $\alpha{+}0.02$ budget per-condition while the pooled means stay within it.

\input{tables/tab_sc3r_k8}
\emph{Replication at $k{=}8$.} Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-k8} repeats the protocol at $k{=}8$ (floor $1/9\approx0.111$), where the anchor is silent at $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10\}$. The sub-floor power unlocking replicates and strengthens: matched-condition SC3R reaches mean power 0.547 at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and 0.693 at $\alpha{=}0.10$ (clean/blur/brightness 0.75--0.83) with precision above 0.91, and the hierarchical bootstrap interval for the power gain again excludes zero for every corruption at both sub-floor levels (weakest: Gaussian noise $\alpha{=}0.05$, $[0.05,0.29]$). False-alarm tracking is exact on clean and structured corruptions (0.050--0.053 at $\alpha{=}0.05$) but the pre-registered budget is met only partially: the pooled mean passes at $\alpha{=}0.05$ (0.052) yet misses the $\alpha{+}0.02$ budget marginally at $\alpha{=}0.10$ (0.121) and $\alpha{=}0.20$ (0.230), with no-harm rates 80\%/74\%/81\%, and the exceedances again concentrate in Gaussian noise and JPEG (0.138/0.141 at $\alpha{=}0.10$). At $\alpha{=}0.20$ the anchor over-alarms under exactly these corruptions (0.39/0.38) while SC3R stays at 0.23--0.26. The mechanism behind the residual exceedances is the same one the uniformity tests expose for the anchor, attenuated but not eliminated: Gaussian noise and JPEG inflate normal residuals more heterogeneously across classes than blur or brightness shifts, so a threshold validated on held-out source classes transfers slightly optimistically to targets whose noise response sits in the tail---and the tighter $k{=}8$ subspace widens exactly this spread. Per-condition (rather than pooled) source validation is the natural mitigation and we flag it as future work. We therefore state the SC3R claim with its boundary: the gate passes in full at $k{=}4$ and at $k{=}8$ for $\alpha{=}0.05$, while heavy-tailed corruptions erode the false-alarm budget at higher levels---the same two conditions flagged at $k{=}4$.

\input{tables/tab_sc3r_visa}
\emph{Replication on VisA and cross-dataset source archives.} Table~\ref{tab:sc3r-visa} repeats the full protocol on all 12 label-stratified VisA classes ($k{=}4$, seeds 0--2), with two source pools. With within-VisA sources, the MVTec conclusion replicates: false-alarm rates track nominal closely (0.051/0.095/0.192---at and below nominal at every level, unlike MVTec's Gaussian/JPEG exceedances), sub-floor power is 0.144/0.351 with alarm precision above 0.84, no-harm is 89\%/84\% at the sub-floor levels, and the hierarchical power-gain interval again excludes zero for every corruption at both sub-floor levels (e.g., blur $\alpha{=}0.10$: $[0.21,0.50]$). At $\alpha{=}0.20$ no-harm dips to 78\%, but the failure mode differs from MVTec's: the VisA anchor is itself conservative at its floor, so SC3R occasionally alarms slightly more than an anchor that under-alarms, while remaining below nominal. Replacing the VisA source pool with the 15 MVTec classes---a genuine cross-dataset source archive---answers the transfer question directly: the archive is \emph{conservative}, with false-alarm rates well below nominal (0.013/0.040/0.098), roughly half to two-thirds of the within-dataset power (0.091/0.201/0.415), precision above 0.85, no-harm 93--97\%, and power-gain intervals that still exclude zero for every corruption at both sub-floor levels (weakest: JPEG $\alpha{=}0.05$, $[0.005,0.066]$). Cross-dataset source archives therefore fail safe: when only foreign-category normals are available, SC3R trades power for a false-alarm rate that stays under budget rather than over it.

\subsection{ECE Is Prevalence-Sensitive for Conformal Scores}
A further protocol warning emerged from prevalence stress tests: ECE computed on $1-p$ conformal scores changes drastically with the anomaly prevalence of the evaluation set even when the ranking is fixed. On full VisA, LOIO ECE moves from 0.404 at 1\% prevalence to 0.149 at 50\%, and the LOIO-versus-weighted ranking reverses across this range. ECE on anomaly-balanced test sets therefore cannot certify deployment reliability of conformal-derived scores. We report ECE as a secondary metric only and rely on false-alarm rate, power, precision, and risk--coverage for operational claims. The risk--coverage view (Figure~\ref{fig:risk-coverage}) also quantifies the value of abstention: ranking images by predictive entropy and abstaining on the most uncertain 20\% halves the remaining LOIO calibration error on full MVTec (0.068 to 0.030) and improves it moderately on VisA (0.077 to 0.075 at 80\% coverage, 0.066 at 70\%), so selective operation is a practical complement to fixed-$\alpha$ alarms.

\subsection{Routing, Pixel Localization, and Fragility Diagnostics}
\input{tables/tab_protocol_routing}
Table~\ref{tab:protocol-routing} evaluates protocol-locked representative routing. Fixed LOIO conformal reduces ECE relative to Vector Platt without using validation labels to select an expert. This supports the central CRR claim: reliability routing can be useful even when the route is fixed and conservative. SAGE-style gating remains a promising design inspiration, but the current main evidence favors simple LOIO routing over learned or weighted expert selection.

\input{tables/tab_pixel_and_diagnostics}
Pixel-level results (Table~\ref{tab:pixel-diagnostics}) show that the low-storage subspace detector is also competitive for localization. Robustness diagnostics are less favorable: FGSM experiments show severe fragility and objective sensitivity. These results are important negative evidence and motivate reliability diagnostics, but they do not support an adversarial robustness claim.
