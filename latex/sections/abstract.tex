\abstract{Few-shot industrial anomaly detection increasingly relies on frozen foundation features such as DINOv2 patch embeddings scored with memory banks or subspace residuals. These detectors rank defects well, but their raw scores are not calibrated probabilities and degrade under low-shot, dataset, and corruption shift. We present \emph{Conformal Reliability Routing} (CRR), a decoupled framework that keeps a low-storage PCA/subspace residual (under 0.6\,MB per class) as the anomaly ranking score and adds a separate label-free reliability layer built from leave-one-image-out (LOIO) conformal p-values computed only from the $k$ normal support images. Because a conformal p-value promises false-alarm control rather than a posterior probability, we evaluate it with conformal-native evidence---false-alarm rates at fixed levels, exact discrete uniformity tests, attainable-alpha analysis, and risk--coverage curves---with calibration error as a secondary, prevalence-sensitive metric. On full corruption benchmarks over all 12 VisA and all 15 MVTec classes, the LOIO route improves calibration over Platt-family and standard scalar calibrators under paired Wilcoxon tests while leaving the ranking unchanged; uniformity tests show target-only p-values are conservative on VisA, anti-conservative on corrupted MVTec, and structurally silent below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$. To alarm below this floor, we introduce SC3R, which pools normal images from other categories and source-validates alarm thresholds under matched conditions. Against a pre-registered decision gate, SC3R tracks nominal false-alarm rates almost exactly on all 15 MVTec classes with bootstrap-supported power gains under every corruption condition, replicates on all 12 VisA classes, and transfers conservatively with cross-dataset source archives. CRR is a calibrated, low-storage reliability layer, not an accuracy or adversarial-robustness claim.}
