\abstract{Few-shot industrial anomaly detection increasingly relies on frozen vision foundation features, especially DINOv2 patch embeddings scored with memory banks or PCA/subspace residuals. These detectors rank defects well, but their raw scores are not calibrated probabilities, and their reliability degrades under low-shot, dataset, and corruption shift. We present \emph{Conformal Reliability Routing} (CRR), a decoupled framework that keeps a low-storage PCA/subspace residual (below 0.6\,MB of class-specific state) as the anomaly ranking score and adds a separate label-free reliability layer built from vector calibration and leave-one-image-out (LOIO) conformal p-values computed only from the $k$ normal support images. Because a conformal p-value promises false-alarm control rather than a posterior probability, we evaluate it with conformal-native evidence: empirical false-alarm rates at fixed levels, exact discrete uniformity tests of normal p-values, an attainable-alpha analysis showing that $k$-shot alarms are structurally silent below $\alpha=1/(k{+}1)$, and risk--coverage curves; expected calibration error is reported only as a secondary, prevalence-sensitive metric. On full corruption benchmarks over all 12 VisA and all 15 MVTec classes, the LOIO route improves calibration over Platt-family and standard scalar calibrators (temperature scaling, isotonic regression, histogram binning) under paired Wilcoxon tests while leaving the ranking unchanged, and the uniformity tests localize when its false-alarm promise fails: target-only p-values are conservative on VisA but anti-conservative on corrupted MVTec. To alarm below the target-only floor, we introduce SC3R, a source-validated cross-category thresholding scheme that pools normal images from other categories under matched conditions. Against a pre-registered decision gate on all 15 MVTec classes, SC3R tracks nominal false-alarm rates almost exactly, gains detection power where the target-only anchor cannot alarm at all, and its power gains carry hierarchical bootstrap intervals that exclude zero for every corruption condition. CRR is positioned as a calibrated, low-storage reliability layer for few-shot inspection, not as an accuracy or adversarial-robustness claim.}
